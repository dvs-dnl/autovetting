The 5th-gen Escalade is a massive improvement over the 4th gen — OLED instrument cluster, air ride suspension, independent rear suspension, and a more refined interior. The 6.2L L87 V8 has DFM (Dynamic Fuel Management) which replaces AFM — more cylinders affected, potentially higher lifter risk. Diesel option is smooth and fuel-efficient. Air suspension compressor failure is the most common repair on used examples. Only buy with Cadillac CPO or extended warranty.

Air Suspension

MagneRide / air compressor failure

High Risk at 50k+
The air ride suspension compressor is prone to failure, especially in cold climates. Symptoms: one corner sagging overnight, compressor running constantly.
Park overnight on level ground, check if vehicle sits level in the morning. Any corner low = compressor failing.
Est. cost: $1,200–$2,500 air compressor replacement Source: Escalade air suspension pattern

Engine

6.2L L87 DFM lifter risk

High Risk at 80k+
DFM (10-cylinder deactivation pattern) creates even more lifter stress than AFM. The same tick-at-warm-idle failure pattern applies.
Warm engine fully, listen at idle for any persistent tick.
Est. cost: $3,000–$6,000 lifter service Source: GM DFM pattern

3.0L Duramax Diesel — smooth but needs DEF

Low Risk
Diesel is more efficient and smoother. Requires DEF fluid top-up every ~5k miles. No widespread failures.
Check DEF level. No smoke or emissions warning lights.
Est. cost: Diesel routine maintenance + DEF Source: GM Duramax 3.0 reliability

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

25V-274
6.2L V8 Connecting Rod / Crankshaft - engine failure
2021-2024 Escalade and Escalade ESV with the 6.2L L87 V8 only: connecting-rod and crankshaft manufacturing defects can damage and destroy the engine. Dealer inspects and repairs or replaces it; cars that pass get higher-viscosity oil, a new oil filler cap, a new filter and a revised owner's manual. The 8th VIN digit decides whether this applies at all - an 'L' car is in scope, a 'T' diesel is not. On an 'L' car, confirm by VIN that it was inspected or the engine replaced before any money moves.
24V-797
Diesel Transmission Control Valve - rear wheels lock
2021 Escalade and Escalade ESV with the 3.0L LM2 Duramax only: the transmission control valve can fail and lock the rear wheels. Dealer installs new transmission control module software, and GM covers the transmission repair where that software finds a defective valve. This is the mirror image of 25V-274 - the same 8th VIN digit that puts an 'L' car in the engine campaign puts a 'T' car in this one, and neither vehicle is in both.
21V-739
Fuel Pump Power Control Module - engine stalls
The fuel pump power control module can make the pump fail or run intermittently, interrupting fuel flow and stalling the engine. Dealer replaces the module. An intermittent stall with no stored fuel-pressure fault is the symptom pattern to ask about.
22V-036
Rear Driveshaft Not Properly Heat-Treated
Rear wheel driveshaft assemblies may contain internal parts that were not heat-treated correctly and can fail, causing a sudden loss of drive power. Dealer replaces the left and/or right rear driveshaft assembly.
21V-190
Third-Row Outboard Seat Belts Pinched or Misrouted
Third-row outboard seat belts may be pinched or routed behind the seat-folding mechanism, which damages the webbing until it can break in a crash. Dealer inspects, replaces damaged belts and reroutes them. This one is partly checkable by hand: fold and unfold the third row and watch where the belt runs.
22V-617
Third-Row Buckle Rivet Improperly Formed
The rivet holding the third-row buckle to its bracket may be improperly formed, so it may not restrain an occupant properly. Dealer inspects the rivet heads on both sides and replaces buckle assemblies as needed. Second of two separate third-row belt campaigns on this vehicle - a seller saying the seat-belt recall was done has to say which.
21V-421
Airbag Warning Lamp May Not Alert the Driver
The communications gateway module mishandles a loss of communication with the sensing diagnostic module, so the airbag warning light can illuminate inconsistently. The defect is the warning, not the airbag: a lamp that stays dark can hide a real airbag fault. Dealer or over-the-air software update.
22V-827
Daytime Running Lights Stay Lit With Headlights
2021 Escalade and Escalade ESV among six GM full-size lines, 338,735 vehicles: body-control-module software can leave the daytime running lights on with the headlights, glaring oncoming traffic - an FMVSS 108 failure. Over-the-air body-control-module update; owner letters went out 2022-12-15.
25V-619
Incorrect Diesel Fuel Tank Fitted at a Prior Repair
2021 Escalade with the 3.0L diesel: an earlier fuel-tank replacement may have installed the wrong tank, which can leak in a rollover. Only 12 vehicles across the whole GM campaign, so this is a long shot - but it is a service-history defect rather than a build defect, which means a diesel Escalade that has had tank work done is exactly the one to check. Dealer inspects for the correct tank and replaces it; owner letters went out 2025-11-05.

What the VIN tells you

8th VIN digit engine code: ‘L’ = 6.2L L87 V8 (420 hp), ‘T’ = 3.0L LM2 Duramax turbodiesel I6 (277 hp). Both confirmed against NHTSA vPIC pattern data. This one digit selects between two entirely different failure families, and it is worth more than any trim badge on the tailgate. ‘L’ cars run Dynamic Fuel Management, which can deactivate any of the eight cylinders rather than AFM's fixed four — more lifters in play, and a collapsed lifter is the expensive outcome. Warm the engine fully, then listen at idle and at a light-throttle 1,200–1,500 rpm for a persistent tick, and ask for service history showing whether lifters, camshaft or the DFM-disabling calibration have already been addressed. ‘T’ cars have no cylinder deactivation, so the lifter question simply does not apply; the diesel-specific checks replace it — confirm the DPF and DEF system are healthy and fault-free, ask how the truck was driven (a short-trip life means incomplete regens and a loaded filter), and check oil-change history against the diesel interval rather than the gas one. Air-ride compressor wear applies to both. Run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov to confirm all open campaigns are closed.
