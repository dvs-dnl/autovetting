The 2017 Rogue is the 5th most popular used vehicle in America — and carries one asterisk that erases everything else if you ignore it: the Jatco JF017E CVT fails at a well-documented rate between 60,000 and 110,000 miles, and replacement costs $4,000–$6,500. Power Train / CVT Transmission

CVT Transmission — Primary Risk

CVT shudder, RPM rubber-band, or whine (JF017E)

Critical — Walk-Away Risk
The Jatco JF017E CVT is the defining risk on the T32 Rogue. NHTSA received over 1,800 complaints on 2014–2020 Rogues specifically about CVT shudder, RPM spike-and-fall at light throttle, and outright failure. CarComplaints aggregated data puts average failure onset at 63,150 miles, with outliers from 37,000 to 109,000 miles. Replacement costs $4,000–$6,500 at most shops. Unlike the Altima (where Nissan extended the warranty under regulatory pressure), the Rogue CVT issue was never recalled — coverage came through a class-action settlement with extended warranty terms that vary by VIN and prior-owner participation.
Accelerate briskly from 20 to 65 mph and feel for shudder, RPM spike without matching acceleration, or hesitation at any speed. Then hold steady at 40 mph on flat road with light throttle for 30 seconds — the CVT should feel glassy-smooth, not jittery. Any shudder, belt-slip sensation, or RPM rubber-banding = CVT wear in progress. Also listen for a whine or moan from under the hood at highway cruise.
Est. cost: $4,000–$6,500 CVT replacement (remanufactured) / $2,500–$3,500 fluid flush + valve body service if caught early Source: NHTSA 1,847 CVT complaints on T32 Rogue; CarComplaints T32 failure-mileage data

CVT fluid condition — NS-3 required

Critical — Pre-failure Indicator
Nissan specifies NS-3 CVT fluid (transparent red/pink). Dark brown, burnt-smelling, or opaque fluid indicates the CVT fluid has never been changed or the transmission has already begun to degrade internally. Many dealerships told 2014–2017 Rogue owners the CVT fluid was “lifetime” — that guidance has been widely acknowledged as incorrect. Fluid condition is the single best pre-purchase predictor of remaining CVT life.
With the engine fully warmed, pull the CVT dipstick (passenger side, near firewall) — fluid should be translucent red/pink and not smell burnt. Dark brown or opaque fluid that smells like burnt rubber = the CVT has been running degraded fluid for an extended period. Do not buy without a fluid analysis or major price concession.
Est. cost: $150–$250 NS-3 fluid service / $4,000–$6,500 CVT replacement if fluid is already contaminated Source: Nissan CVT NS-3 service specification; Rogue owner documentation

CVT extended warranty / class-action coverage

High Attention
Nissan settled a class-action covering JF017E CVT failures on the 2014–2018 Rogue and Rogue Sport. Extended warranty terms vary by model year and by whether the prior owner registered for the settlement. Some 2017 Rogues may still be within the extended coverage window (commonly 84 months / 84,000 miles from original in-service date). A Rogue still inside coverage is materially more valuable than one outside it.
Call Nissan Customer Affairs (1-800-647-7261) with the VIN and ask: (1) Is there CVT extended warranty coverage on this VIN? (2) What is the remaining term? Get the answer in writing before signing. A dealer who says “no extended coverage” without checking the specific VIN is guessing.
Est. cost: $0 if within coverage; $4,000–$6,500 if outside Source: Nissan T32 Rogue CVT class-action settlement documentation

Engine — QR25DE 2.5L

Oil consumption and milky cap check

Monitor
The QR25DE in the Rogue is generally better-controlled than in the Altima, but higher-mileage examples with extended oil change intervals can develop mild consumption. A milky or frothy oil cap is an immediate red flag (coolant contamination = head gasket concern).
Pull the dipstick — should be within ½ quart of full at any point in the oil change interval. Remove the oil filler cap and inspect the underside for milky residue (white/tan = coolant contamination; walk away). Ask when oil was last changed.
Est. cost: $0–$50 if mild / $2,500–$4,500 head gasket service if milky Source: Nissan QR25DE consumption documentation; Rogue owner reports

Cold-start timing chain rattle (high-mileage note)

Low Risk (note)
The QR25DE timing chain system is more robust than the Altima’s, but on high-mileage or oil-neglected examples a brief cold-start rattle from the chain tensioner can develop. Uncommon on well-maintained Rogues under 150k miles — note it, do not treat as primary risk.
Cold-start the engine and listen for any tick or rattle in the first 5 seconds. A brief tick fading within 2 seconds is borderline normal at very high mileage. Persistent rattle past 5 seconds warrants mechanic evaluation.
Est. cost: $500–$1,000 timing chain service if needed Source: Nissan QR25DE documentation; Rogue vs. Altima failure-rate comparison

ADAS & Electrical

AEB (Automatic Emergency Braking) false activations

High Attention
Unexpected hard braking events at highway speed — AEB triggering without an obstacle — are among the top owner complaints for 2017–2019 Rogues. Nissan issued software reflashes that improved but did not fully eliminate the issue.
Verify no AEB warning lights are illuminated on the instrument cluster during the test drive. Ask whether the latest AEB software update has been applied at a Nissan dealer (check via VIN at a service center). On the test drive, note any unexpected brake-pull at highway speeds.
Est. cost: $0 for software update at Nissan dealer / $500–$1,500 AEB sensor replacement if hardware failure Source: NHTSA AEB complaint data on 2017–2019 T32 Rogue; Nissan reflash campaigns

Electric power steering module failure

Monitor
The EPS module can develop intermittent failures — sudden heavy steering, steering warning light, or moaning at low speed. Most common on higher-mileage examples.
Cycle the steering wheel slowly lock-to-lock at parking-lot speed. Steering should be light and consistent throughout. Sudden increase in resistance, grinding, or a warning light during the test = EPS module inspection needed.
Est. cost: $400–$900 EPS module replacement Source: Nissan T32 Rogue EPS owner reports

TPMS sensor premature failure

Low Risk (leverage)
Tire pressure monitoring sensors on the T32 Rogue are documented for premature failure — often before 60,000 miles. A TPMS light is not a dealbreaker but is price leverage.
Note any TPMS warning light on the dashboard. If present, factor $60–$90 per sensor (4 sensors = $240–$360 installed) into your offer.
Est. cost: $60–$90 per sensor installed Source: Nissan T32 Rogue TPMS complaint pattern

Suspension, Drivetrain & Body

AWD rear differential / coupling service (AWD models)

High Attention
The T32 Rogue uses an on-demand AWD system with an electronically-controlled rear coupling. Rear differential fluid requires a drain-and-fill around 60,000 miles — a service routinely skipped on used inventory. Whining or groaning from the rear at highway speed = degraded fluid or coupling wear. Full coupling replacement: $800–$1,200.
Accelerate to 60–65 mph and listen for a whine from the rear of the vehicle. On a lift, inspect the rear differential drain plug area for weeping fluid. Ask for documentation of rear differential fluid service — absence of records = factor $100–$150 service into the offer immediately.
Est. cost: $100–$150 fluid service / $800–$1,200 coupling replacement if degraded Source: Nissan T32 AWD coupling service requirement; owner reports

Front wheel bearing hum

Monitor
Front wheel bearing failures appear somewhat earlier than expected on the T32 Rogue, typically between 80,000 and 120,000 miles. The symptom is a low-frequency hum or growl that changes with vehicle speed (not engine speed), sometimes shifting with lane changes.
At 55–65 mph, listen for a hum that rises and falls with vehicle speed. On a highway on-ramp, sway the vehicle gently left and right — if the hum shifts with lateral load, that is a wheel bearing. A lift inspection for play confirms it.
Est. cost: $250–$500 per wheel bearing replacement Source: Nissan T32 Rogue wheel bearing failure pattern

Underbody / salt-belt corrosion

High Attention
Northeast and Upper Midwest Rogues accumulate corrosion on the rear subframe mounting points, exhaust hangers, and brake line routing. The aluminum appearance of body panels can mislead — the frame structure underneath them is steel and rusts. A Phoenix car and a Buffalo car are not the same risk.
On a lift, inspect the rear subframe mounting bosses, exhaust hangers, and brake line clips for scaling or pitting rust. Surface rust (uniform orange-brown) is cosmetic. Flaking, scaling, or pinholes in structural metal = genuine concern. Cross-reference with the vehicle’s title state.
Est. cost: $500–$2,500 depending on extent / $0 on Southern/Southwestern cars Source: T32 Rogue salt-belt owner documentation

HVAC blower motor and mode actuator

Monitor
Blower motor failures (fan speed 1–2 stops working before higher speeds) and HVAC mode actuator clicking are moderately common on the T32 Rogue by 80,000–100,000 miles.
Cycle the blower through every fan speed from 1 to max. Dead low speeds = worn blower motor resistor. Switch between every vent mode (face, floor, defrost) and listen for clicking from the dash. Clicking = mode actuator gear wear.
Est. cost: $80–$200 blower resistor / $100–$300 mode actuator at a shop Source: T32 Rogue HVAC complaint documentation

Open Recalls — Verify All Before Purchase

Recall 22V-875 — Dash harness corrosion (fire risk)

Critical — Fire Risk
NHTSA recall 22V-875 covers approximately 125,000 units of the 2017 Nissan Rogue. Water intrusion can corrode a wiring connector under the instrument panel, potentially causing a fire. Dealer remedy is grease application and connector repair — free. If open on a Northeast or coastal car, this is a safety item the prior owner skipped for years.
Run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before the test drive. Campaign 22V-875 should show as "Completed" if the car is an affected unit. Open = free dealer repair required before taking ownership.
Est. cost: Free dealer repair Source: NHTSA recall 22V-875 (2017 Nissan Rogue dash harness corrosion)

Recalls 23V-093, 17V-663, 16V-244 — additional active campaigns

High Attention
Three additional campaigns affect a significant share of 2017 Rogue production: 23V-093 (folding key collapses mid-drive, engine shuts off — jackknife key spacer fix), 17V-663 (2016–2017 builds: rear seat recliner welds may fail in a crash), and 16V-244 (2014–2016 builds: passenger frontal airbag may be disabled due to OCS sensor error — software reflash). Any open safety campaign is free dealer work the prior owner skipped.
At nhtsa.gov/recalls, confirm all three campaign IDs show "Completed" on the specific VIN. Each open campaign is a negotiation line-item or a condition of purchase.
Est. cost: Free dealer repair for all three Source: NHTSA recalls 23V-093, 17V-663, 16V-244 (2017 Nissan Rogue)

What the VIN tells you

8th VIN digit engine code: ‘A’ = 2.5L QR25DE I4 (all standard 2014–2020 Rogue). The Rogue Hybrid (2017–2019) uses the MR20 engine; 8th digit ‘B’ indicates the hybrid drivetrain and requires a completely different inspection protocol — hybrid battery SOH, regenerative brake system, and inverter cooling pump (not covered here). If the VIN decodes to a Hybrid, do not apply this checklist.
