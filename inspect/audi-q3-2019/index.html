2019 Audi Q3
Premium / Premium Plus / Prestige (F3, 2nd gen, US 2019-2025; the 8U 1st gen ends with 2018)
Monitor

The 2nd-gen Q3 (F3) is a well-sized entry luxury crossover — better proportioned than the 8U it replaced, with a much-improved interior. 2019 is its first US model year, so treat it as a first-year car. US-market cars are the 228 hp 45 TFSI with quattro standard; the 184 hp figure quoted in a lot of online spec tables is the European 35 TFSI, which was never sold here. The EA888 2.0T is a proven engine across VW Group. Main risks: timing chain rattle from oil neglect, oil consumption (same EA888 pattern as Q5/Atlas), and Audi maintenance costs. Only buy with documented Audi/VAG-certified shop history. On recalls, NHTSA lists four campaigns for MY2019, and one of them is live right now: 26V-590, a corroding steering-rack-to-subframe bolt that can break the rack housing and cause a loss of steering control. It was reported 2026-09-11 and owner letters are not due until 2026-11-10, so assume it is open on any 2019 Q3 you look at and confirm it at an Audi dealer before you buy.

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

26V-590
Severity: Critical — open, letters not yet mailed
The right-side bolt attaching the steering rack to the subframe can corrode and break, which can break the steering rack housing and cause a loss of steering control. 208,724 units across the 2018 Tiguan, 2018-2019 Atlas and 2019-2021 Q3; Audi 48LG and 48VT; report received 2026-09-11 and owner letters are not due until 2026-11-10 — so this is open on effectively every 2019 Q3 on the market today. Remedy: right-side steering rack mounting bolt replaced free.
22V-815
Severity: High — FMVSS 138 noncompliance
The tire pressure monitoring system may not warn in time when all four tires lose pressure at once, so the vehicle does not meet FMVSS 138. 296,044 units across many 2019 Volkswagen Group models, Audi 45J7; owner letters mailed 2022-11-18. Remedy: TPMS software updated free.
20V-273
Severity: High — passenger airbag
The passenger airbag may not deploy correctly through the instrument panel tear seam, and plastic fragments can enter the cabin. 3,024 units, Volkswagen 70H7, began 2020-06-10. Remedy: instrument panel inspected and replaced if needed, free.
21V-027
Severity: Informational — 1 vehicle
A belt pulley production deviation can leave the steering rack circlip unseated; if it detaches it can jam adjacent parts and lock the steering. The population is a single vehicle, Audi 48P7, and the campaign was completed 2021-02-28 — recorded for completeness rather than as a live risk. Remedy: steering rack replaced free.

What the VIN tells you

The 2019 Q3 gives a buyer no engine fork at all, so the useful decode here is the generation check instead. Verified against NHTSA vPIC: off WA1AECF3*K1, position 8 at ‘3’ returns Audi / Q3 / 2019 / quattro Premium / 2.0L / 4 cylinders / 228 hp / AWD, and changing only that character to ‘4’ or ‘8’ drops Model, displacement and every engine field out of the response entirely (error 14, with vPIC suggesting a correction back). Real-world 2019 VINs agree — WA1AECF30K1077709 and WA1AECF39K1075599 both carry ‘3’ in that position — so one engine, one character, nothing to shop between. Position 4 carries market and trim rather than hardware: ‘A’ is the US Premium, ‘F’ the Canadian Technik. The fork that matters is F3 versus 8U, and it is measurable: a 2018 8U VIN, WA1BCCFS7JR034731, decodes clean to Q3 / 2018 / Premium, Sport Premium / 2.0L / 200 hp, reading ‘CCFS’ at positions 4–7 where this 2019 F3 reads ‘AECF’ and 228 hp. They are different cars — different platform, different interior, first model year here — so a decode returning 200 hp means the items below do not apply to the car in front of you. The 184 hp figure in a lot of online spec tables is the European 35 TFSI and was never sold here; anything other than 2.0L / 228 hp / AWD on a car being sold as a 2019 Q3 should be re-identified before you use this checklist.
