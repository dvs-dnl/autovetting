2019 Genesis G70
2.0T / 3.3T Sport / Sport Advanced (1st gen IK, 2019–2021)
Low Risk

The G70 is Genesis' BMW 3 Series competitor — rear-wheel drive, optional AWD, excellent driving dynamics, and premium interior at a significant discount to German rivals. No chronic powertrain failures. The 3.3T is the standout choice. The 8-speed DCT on some trims can shudder; the 8-speed automatic is smooth. Genesis is relatively new so certified pre-owned with extended Genesis warranty coverage is preferred. Independent shop support is limited. On recalls, NHTSA lists five campaigns for MY2019 and two are park-outside fire campaigns - 21V-161 (ABS module short, 552 cars) and 24V-107 (water in the starter solenoid, 90,907) - so confirm both by VIN before buying. Which of the two powertrain campaigns applies depends on the engine rather than the year: 24V-191 (left turbocharger oil-feed pipe) is explicitly 3.3L twin-turbo V6 only, while 24V-528 (fuel-pump failure, loss of drive power) is the campaign shared with Hyundai's 2.0T N cars.

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

21V-161
Severity: Critical - park outside, fire risk
The ABS module can malfunction and short-circuit, which can start an engine-compartment fire whether the car is parked or being driven. NHTSA flags this one park-outside. Only 552 units across the 2019-2021 G70, so it is VIN-specific; report received 2021-03-10. Remedy: ABS module fuse replaced free by a dealer. Park away from structures until it is done.
24V-107
Severity: Critical - park outside, fire risk
Water can enter the starter solenoid and cause an electrical short, starting an engine-compartment fire parked or driving. NHTSA flags this one park-outside. 90,907 units across 2015-2016 Genesis, 2017-2019 G80 and G90 and the 2019 G70; report received 2024-02-13. Remedy: a remedy relay kit installed in the engine junction box, free.
24V-191
Severity: Critical - 3.3T only, engine-bay fire
The left turbocharger oil-feed pipe can deteriorate and leak oil onto hot engine parts, raising the risk of an engine-compartment fire. This campaign covers only cars with the 3.3L twin-turbo V6, which is why the 8th VIN digit matters more than the model year here - a 2.0T G70 is outside it. 28,439 units across the 2019-2022 G70, 2018-2020 G80 and 2017-2022 G90; report received 2024-03-08, owner letters mailed 2024-05-03, Hyundai number 019G. It expands and replaces 19V-538, the 2019 campaign for the same pipe - but 19V-538 covered only the 2017-2018 G90 and 2018 G80, no G70 at all, so for a 2019 G70 this is first-time exposure rather than a repeat visit. Remedy: left turbo oil-feed pipe replaced free.
24V-528
Severity: High - loss of drive power
The fuel pump may fail and cause a loss of drive power. 54,647 units across the 2019-2023 G70 and Hyundai's 2019-2022 Veloster N and 2022-2023 Elantra N and Kona N - all 2.0T Theta II cars, though NHTSA's record states no engine restriction for the G70 itself, so confirm by VIN whichever engine you are looking at. Report received 2024-07-12; interim safety-risk letters mailed 2024-09-03 and owner letters from 2024-09-09; Hyundai numbers 262 and 023G. Remedy: engine control module software updated and the fuel pump assembly inspected and replaced as needed, free.
22V-196
Severity: Monitor - trunk cannot be opened from inside
The trunk latch can be damaged so the trunk cannot be opened from inside, which fails FMVSS 401 and raises the injury risk to anyone shut in there. 63,461 units across the 2019 G70 and the 2020 Sonata and Sonata Hybrid; report received 2022-03-28. Remedy: trunk latch inspected and the latch base replaced as necessary, free.

What the VIN tells you

The 8th VIN digit is decisive on the G70 because its two engines are mechanically unrelated. 'A' = 2.0L Theta II T-GDI turbo four (252 hp); 'E' = 3.3L Lambda II twin-turbo V6 (365 hp), both with an 8-speed automatic. Note the 'Lambda' badge applies only to the 3.3T V6 — the 2.0T is a Theta II engine, a different family with its own service history — so read the 8th digit, not the trim name, to know which you are buying. A 3.3T is the enthusiast pick and commands a premium; a 2.0T is the value daily driver. Independent-shop support is thin either way, so favor examples with remaining Genesis powertrain warranty, and run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov to confirm any open recall for that specific car is closed.
