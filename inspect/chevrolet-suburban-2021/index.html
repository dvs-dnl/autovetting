What the VIN tells you

8th VIN digit engine code on the 5th-gen T1XX Suburban: ‘D’ = 5.3L L84 V8, ‘L’ = 6.2L L87 V8, ‘T’ = 3.0L LM2 Duramax turbodiesel I6. Verified on a Suburban VIN rather than inherited from the Tahoe: a constructed 2021 Suburban VIN was decoded through NHTSA vPIC three times with position 8 as the only variable, and vPIC returned L84, L87 and LM2 in turn. That is what licenses the wider claim — the encoding is platform-wide across GM’s T1XX full-size SUVs, so the same digit reads the same way on a Tahoe, a Yukon or a Yukon XL. Read it before the tailgate badge, because it decides which inspection you are doing. ‘D’ and ‘L’ are both Dynamic Fuel Management cylinder-deactivation engines, so a gas Suburban is a lifter car either way: warm it fully, then listen at idle and at a light-throttle 1,200–1,500 rpm for a persistent tick, and ask for records showing whether lifters, camshaft or a DFM-disabling calibration have already been done. ‘T’ has no cylinder deactivation, so the lifter question does not apply to the diesel; diesel-specific checks replace it — confirm the DPF and DEF system are fault-free, ask how the truck was driven, because a short-trip life means incomplete regens and a loaded filter, and check oil-change history against the diesel interval rather than the gas one. One caution on reading vPIC output: its descriptor text spells out DFM only on the L87 string, a labelling quirk of the database rather than evidence that an L84 car lacks it. Air-ride compressor wear applies to all three engines, and on the Suburban the long-wheelbase body makes the rear air springs and their lines the part to look at hardest. Run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov to confirm every open campaign is closed.
