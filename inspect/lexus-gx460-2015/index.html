Recall — Check First

Takata inflator recall — this one is real, verify it is closed

Critical — Verify Closed
Lexus lists the 2010–2017 GX460 among its Takata-affected models (2017 is partial), and this is the only recall on this truck that is a walk-away if it is still open — a degraded inflator can rupture and drive metal fragments into the cabin. Involvement is assigned by VIN and build date, not by model year alone, and cars first sold or registered in high-absolute-humidity states were pulled into the campaign earlier.
Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and require the inflator campaign to read as completed. Do not accept a verbal assurance — remedy completion is recorded against the VIN. Parts are available and the repair, including transport, is free.
Est. cost: Free under recall Source: Toyota Takata affected-model list (toyota.com/recall/takata), verified 2026-08-09

Powertrain

4.6L 1UR-FE V8 — bulletproof

Low Risk
Toyota's 1UR-FE is one of the most reliable V8 engines built. Timing chain is robust; no chronic failure modes. Check for valve cover seeps on high-mileage units.
Check oil color and level. Look under hood for valve cover seeps. Cold-start idle should be smooth and quiet.
Est. cost: Routine maintenance Source: Toyota/Lexus 1UR-FE reliability data

Low Risk
The A760F/H is extremely durable. Change Toyota WS fluid every 60k miles.
Smooth shifts in all gears including tow mode. No hesitation or slip.

Rear differential — fluid service

Monitor
Rear diff and transfer case fluid should be changed every 30k miles. Neglected fluid leads to bearing noise and eventual failure.
Ask for differential service records. Request service if unknown.
Est. cost: $200–$350 full drivetrain fluid service Source: Lexus GX460 service schedule

Underbody & Suspension

Frame and underbody rust

High Risk (rust belt)
GX460 frames rust in northern salt states. Less severe than Tacoma/4Runner but still worth inspecting.
Inspect frame rails and skid plates from below with a flashlight. Surface oxidation is normal; perforation is not.
Est. cost: Varies Source: AutoVetting editorial

Kinetic Dynamic Suspension System (KDSS) — if equipped

Monitor
KDSS hydraulic stabilizer bars can develop leaks at high mileage ($1,500–$3,500 to service). Check for any fluid on the stabilizer bar actuators.
Inspect KDSS hydraulic actuators at each axle for oil seepage.
Est. cost: $1,500–$3,500 Source: Lexus GX owner pattern

What the VIN tells you

The 5th VIN digit reads ‘M’ on every GX460 ever built. The 4.6L 1UR-FE V8 is the only engine offered across the entire 2010–2022 J150 run, so the VIN cannot narrow the powertrain and there is no engine-code homework to do here. What the VIN must confirm is that this is a GX460 and not a GX470. The GX470 (2003–2009, J120) uses the 4.7L 2UZ-FE, and that engine is timing-BELT driven — a 90,000-mile belt-and-water-pump service that typically quotes $1,200–$2,000 and is a walk-away if it is undocumented and overdue. The 1UR-FE in the GX460 is timing-CHAIN driven with no such interval. Listings and sellers use “GX” loosely, so decode the full VIN at autovetting.com/decode and confirm the 460 before you price the truck or book a shop. Two things the VIN will NOT tell you, both of which change what you inspect: KDSS (the Kinetic Dynamic Suspension System, fitted to Premium and Luxury) is not reliably encoded — look under the truck for the hydraulic accumulator cylinders at the sway bars and check for weeping, because KDSS work is expensive and the system is not something you retrofit; and Takata inflator involvement is assigned by VIN and build date, not by model year, so run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls rather than reasoning from the year alone.
