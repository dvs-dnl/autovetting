Low Risk

The XK60 Sequoia is one of the most durable full-size SUVs ever made. The 5.7L 3UR-FE is bulletproof with regular maintenance. The main buyer concerns are frame rust (critical in rust-belt states), open-recall status, and timing chain service history — the Takata inflator recall is not among them, since Toyota's Sequoia scope is the 2002–2007 first-generation truck. Southern-state cars are far better buys than northern ones. Expect 200k–300k miles from a well-maintained example.

Suspension / Frame (rust belt)

Frame & Underbody

Frame rust — critical in rust-belt states

High Risk (rust belt)
Toyota truck frames from this era are prone to severe frame rust in states that use road salt (Northeast, Midwest). In severe cases the frame can perforate, making the vehicle unsafe and unrestorable. Southern-state cars rarely have this issue.
Jack the vehicle and inspect frame rails from front to rear with a flashlight. Surface rust = monitor; perforation or bubbling = walk away.
Est. cost: $0–5,000 (coating) or totaling the vehicle if perforated Source: Toyota truck frame rust pattern / Toyota settlement program (earlier gen overlap)

Exhaust manifold cracks (V8)

Monitor
The 3UR-FE exhaust manifolds are cast iron and can develop hairline cracks over time, causing a ticking sound at cold start.
Listen for ticking at cold start that disappears when warm. Inspect manifold for soot trails.
Est. cost: $400–$800 per manifold Source: Toyota V8 common pattern

Safety Recall

Open recalls — check VIN (the Sequoia Takata recall is the first-gen truck)

Monitor
Toyota's Sequoia Takata scope is 2002–2007 — the first-generation truck. The XK60 is not in it, on either the nationwide or the humidity-zone campaign. If a listing claims a Takata repair was done on a 2016 Sequoia, the paperwork belongs to a different vehicle.
Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before you go and see the car. Any campaign that comes back open = do not purchase until the dealer has closed it. Recall work is always free, and completion is logged against the VIN, so a seller's word is not evidence.
Est. cost: Free under recall Source: Toyota Takata affected-model list (toyota.com/recall/takata), verified 2026-08-09

Powertrain

5.7L 3UR-FE engine health

Low Risk
One of Toyota’s most reliable engines. Timing chain is robust but should be inspected on high-mileage units (120k+). Check for any oil leaks at valve covers.
Standard oil/coolant check. Timing chain rattle on cold start = service needed. Leaks at valve covers are common on high-mileage units.
Est. cost: $300–$800 valve cover gaskets; timing chain varies Source: Toyota V8 reliability history

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

VIN check
Severity: Informational
The XK60 Sequoia is not in a Takata inflator campaign — Toyota's Sequoia scope is the 2002–2007 first-generation truck. Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls for the campaigns that do apply. Remedy: Any open campaign is repaired free by a dealer.

What the VIN tells you

Toyota encodes the engine in the 5th VIN digit, not the 8th. ‘Y’ = 5.7L 3UR-FE gasoline V8 (381 hp). ‘W’ = 5.7L 3UR-FBE — the flex-fuel, E85-capable version of the same V8. Mechanically they are the same engine, so none of the frame-rust, timing-chain, or exhaust-manifold guidance below changes. But the flex-fuel car carries one failure pattern the gasoline car does not, and it is the single most useful thing this digit tells you. Toyota TSB T-SB-0166-19 covers 2009–2018 Sequoia (and 2009–2019 Tundra) 3UR-FBE engines for extended crank / hard start and a check-engine light with DTC P0171, P0174, P0172, P0175, or P1604 — the ECM learns a fuel alcohol density that does not match what is actually in the tank. The repair is not a reflash alone: Toyota’s published procedure is an ECM reprogram PLUS a fuel pump assembly replacement PLUS a fuel-density learning reset, a 2.2-hour dealer operation. It was covered by the Federal Emission Warranty for 96 months / 80,000 miles from the in-service date, which has expired on every 2016 — so on a ‘W’ truck this is customer-pay, and it is a fuel pump plus better than two hours of labor. Test for it yourself: insist on a genuinely cold start (arrive early, do not let the seller warm it up first) and count the crank — a long crank on a flex-fuel Sequoia is the tell. Scan for stored P0171/P0174 even with the light off, and ask whether the truck carries the Toyota Authorized Modifications label that dealers affix when this calibration has been completed. A ‘Y’ truck needs none of this. Decode the full VIN at autovetting.com/decode before applying this checklist.
