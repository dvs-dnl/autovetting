Watchful

Z52 Murano is Nissan's most premium crossover with a genuinely upscale interior. The 3.5L VQ35DE V6 is one of Nissan's most proven engines — the CVT remains the risk vector. CVT service at 30k miles with genuine NS-3 fluid is mandatory. Body on Frame alternative? No — this is unibody-only. Strong buy for buyers who prioritize comfort and have documented service history.

What the VIN tells you

The 2018 Murano has one engine, the 3.5L VQ35DE, so no VIN character can tell you anything about it - vPIC returns displacement 3.5 on every pattern. What the 8th digit carries is the drivetrain, and on a CVT car that changes the inspection. Single-variable decode at vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov, holding 5N1AZ2M and the JN tail constant and changing only position 8: 'G' returns DriveType 4x2, 'H' returns 4WD/4-Wheel Drive/4x4, and every other field - make, model, 3.5L, Canton assembly - comes back identical. Full VINs confirm it: 5N1AZ2MG0JN154091 decodes clean as a front-driver and was advertised by its selling dealer as a Platinum FWD, while 5N1AZ2MH4JN104495 decodes as 4x4. Both characters turn up on SV and on Platinum cars, so this is drivetrain, not trim. Note that NHTSA labels position 8 unused when you hand it a pattern it cannot resolve, which is why a partial decode can make the character look meaningless when it is in fact telling you whether the car is all-wheel drive. An H car adds a propeller shaft, a rear differential and the AWD coupling to the pre-purchase inspection, and gives the Xtronic CVT more to pull, so the fluid-history demand in the first powertrain item is stricter on an AWD Murano than on a front-driver. Run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov before purchase; open campaigns on this generation vary by build date. Observed and not changed here: the stats block says one recall, the documentation item says three are possible, and the recalls array carries a Multiple placeholder - that is recall-ledger work, not VIN work.
