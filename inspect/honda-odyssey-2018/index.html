High Attention

The 5th-gen Odyssey (2018–2023) is a sophisticated minivan with one serious documented flaw: oil dilution. On some examples driven predominantly on short trips, raw gasoline enters the engine oil through the cylinder walls, dramatically reducing lubrication quality. Honda acknowledged the issue and extended the powertrain warranty to 8 years / 80,000 miles for affected builds. The 9-speed automatic in early 2018 production had calibration issues; later 2018s and 2019+ moved to a smoother 10-speed. A well-maintained 2019+ Odyssey with full service records is a strong buy; a 2018 with incomplete service history requires extra scrutiny on the engine.

Engine — Oil Dilution

Gasoline-in-oil dilution — Honda extended warranty issue

Critical
On some 5th-gen Odysseys driven primarily on short trips (under 10 miles), combustion blow-by allows raw gasoline to accumulate in the engine oil. The oil looks normal visually but a diluted oil film lubricates far less effectively. Honda issued TSB 19-087 and extended the powertrain warranty to 8 years / 80,000 miles. 2019+ models received ECM updates that partially mitigated the issue.
Pull the oil dipstick and smell the oil. A faint gasoline smell is the primary indicator — regular motor oil has no gas smell. Ask the seller about typical driving patterns: predominantly short trips under 10 miles is high-risk. Ask if the Honda extended warranty oil-dilution coverage was ever invoked at a dealer.
Est. cost: $0 if covered by Honda extended warranty / $5,000–$8,000 engine work out of warranty Source: Honda TSB 19-087 + Honda oil dilution extended warranty

VCM cylinder deactivation — motor mount wear

High Attention
Like the Accord V6 and Pilot, the Odyssey 3.5L V6 uses VCM. The cylinder transition vibration prematurely wears the right-rear motor mount, typically by 80,000–100,000 miles.
With engine running in Drive, foot on brake, hood open: watch for visible engine rocking. Check if a VCM Muzzler or similar device has been installed (positive sign of a proactive owner). A mechanic on a lift should compress the right-rear mount — it should resist firmly.
Est. cost: $400–$700 right-rear mount Source: Honda VCM motor mount pattern

Oil change interval compliance — critical on this engine

High Attention
Because of the oil dilution risk, Honda updated the Odyssey Maintenance Minder to recommend oil changes at 5,000 miles or less for short-trip driving patterns. Sellers who followed the Honda default 7,500-mile interval on a short-trip vehicle may have run diluted oil longer than advisable.
Request every oil change receipt. Look for service intervals: gaps over 6 months or 6,000 miles on a short-trip vehicle = elevated dilution risk. A used-oil sample test (about $30) can quantify dilution level.
Est. cost: $0 if intervals are compliant / $5,000–$8,000 engine consequences if not Source: Honda Maintenance Minder update post-TSB 19-087

Transmission

9-speed automatic (early 2018) — calibration issues

High Attention
Early 2018 Odyssey builds launched with a 9-speed automatic that exhibited hunting, harsh downshifts, and hesitation in stop-and-go driving — the same class of issue as the Pilot 2016–2017. Honda issued software updates and shifted to a 10-speed in later 2018 production and all 2019+. Check the door jamb build date: early 2018 builds are higher risk.
Test drive: gradual acceleration from 5–35 mph in light traffic, and highway cruise at 55–70 mph on a gently rolling road. Watch for hesitation at tip-in and unprompted downshifts on grades. Ask a Honda dealer (free VIN check) if all transmission software updates have been applied.
Est. cost: $0 software update / $2,500–$5,000 valve body if mechanical damage Source: Honda Odyssey 5th gen transmission documentation + NHTSA complaints

10-speed automatic (2019+, later 2018) — reliable baseline

Low Risk
The revised 10-speed is substantially smoother than the early 9-speed. Failures are uncommon. Standard ATF-DW1 fluid maintenance applies.
Verify ATF has been serviced per Honda schedule (30k miles). Test drive should show clean progression through all ranges.
Est. cost: $180 fluid service / $2,000–$4,000 rebuild if neglected Source: Honda 10-speed AT documentation

Sliding Doors & Interior

Power sliding door motors and tracks

Monitor
Minivan sliding doors are high-use mechanisms. On the 5th-gen Odyssey, motor brush wear and track debris are the primary failure modes. Symptoms: slow operation, mid-travel hesitation, failure to latch on first closure attempt.
Cycle each sliding door fully open and closed five times. Test from the door handle, the B-pillar button, the dash switch, and the key fob. Check the track for visible debris or grime that could cause drag.
Est. cost: $400–$800 motor replacement per door / $100 track cleaning Source: Honda Odyssey 5th gen sliding door pattern

Magic Slide second-row seat tracks

Monitor
The Magic Slide second-row seats slide laterally on floor tracks for walk-in access. The tracks can grit up, making lateral movement stiff or binding. Seat-back recline mechanisms also develop play over time.
Slide both second-row seats fully in each lateral direction. Movement should be smooth and require only moderate force. Test all recline positions.
Est. cost: $0 track cleaning / $200–$500 track mechanism repair if binding is mechanical Source: Honda Odyssey Magic Slide documentation

Third-row fold-flat seat condition

Low Risk
Unlike Chrysler minivans, the Odyssey stores the third row folded flat against the cargo floor. The fold mechanism should be tested — cable pulls and latches can stiffen.
Fully fold and unfold the third row. Test all seatbelts. Inspect the cargo floor for water staining near the liftgate seal.
Est. cost: $50–$200 cable or latch repair Source: Standard wear item

CabinWatch / CabinTalk camera (Touring / Elite)

Low Risk
Premium trims include a cabin-facing camera and intercom. Camera lens can cloud over time; intercom can develop static.
Activate CabinWatch on the infotainment screen — image should be clear. Test CabinTalk intercom if equipped.
Est. cost: $200–$500 camera module if clouded Source: Honda Odyssey Elite feature documentation

Safety & Electrical

Honda Sensing false braking (early 5th gen)

Monitor
Honda Sensing is standard on most 5th-gen Odyssey trims. Early builds had false-positive CMBS events — unexpected braking on a clear highway stretch. Honda issued radar and camera calibration software updates.
During a highway test drive, confirm Honda Sensing does not activate on a clear road. Confirm the Sensing icon on the cluster clears cleanly after the startup self-test.
Est. cost: $0 software update / $1,500–$3,000 sensor if replacement needed Source: Honda TSB (Honda Sensing calibration campaigns)

Liftgate hatch struts

Low Risk
At 6–7 years of age, the hydraulic struts that hold the liftgate open lose pressure and let the door fall slowly.
Open the liftgate and let go — it should stay fully open without any tendency to fall.
Est. cost: $80–$160 strut pair Source: Standard wear item

Open recall check — fuel pump and restraint campaigns

Monitor
The 5th-gen Odyssey has had recall campaigns on fuel pump (shared with Pilot/Passport) and restraint systems.
Enter VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. Any open recall = require dealer completion before purchase.
Est. cost: Free dealer repair Source: NHTSA recall database
