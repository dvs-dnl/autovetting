What the VIN tells you

On the K2XX Yukon the 8th VIN digit is the engine, and it changes which transmission you are testing. Single-variable decodes at vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov for model year 2018, on two independent base patterns, 1GKS2BK (4WD) and 1GKS1BK (4x2): C returns L83 - Spark Ignited Direct Injection, Variable Valve Timing, AFM, E85 MAX, ALUM at 5.3L, and J returns L86 at 6.2L. Same answer on both patterns. Published specification says what that character means on the lot: Consumer Guide's 2018 Yukon review states that the base engine in Yukon and Yukon XL is a 5.3-liter V8 with 355 horsepower paired with a 6-speed automatic, and that the topline Denali models get a 420-hp 6.2-liter V8 mated to the 10-speed automatic. The inspection consequence is the gearbox, not the lifters. Both engines use AFM, so the critical lifter item below applies either way, but the 6L80 fluid-shudder test is a 5.3L test: on a J car you are inspecting a 10L80 10-speed and that symptom does not transfer. A J car is also a Denali, which is the trim the rear air-leveling item below is scoped to. Three cautions, all measured. vPIC reports TransmissionSpeeds of 6 on the J pattern as well as the C pattern, which is wrong for the 6.2L - take the gearbox from position 8 plus the published pairing, not from vPIC's transmission field. vPIC returns Trim SLT on the J pattern, a car that was never sold, since the 6.2L was Denali-only; a single-character probe will build combinations GM never shipped. And the allowed character set is narrower here than on the Chevrolet sibling: G, which returns the 6.0L L96 on a Suburban pattern, comes back on a Yukon pattern with error 14 and every engine field blank. Only C and J resolve to an engine on a 2018 Yukon. One cross-reference, because this catalog carries both generations: the 2021 Yukon entry maps position 8 as D, L and T for the L84, L87 and LM2 Duramax on the T1XX platform. K2XX and T1XX share a badge and nothing about their position-8 map, so a reading does not carry across the 2020/2021 line.
