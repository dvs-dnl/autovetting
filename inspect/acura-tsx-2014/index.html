Powertrain

K24Z3 engine condition (the headline)

No Known Issues
The K24Z3 is one of the most reliable 4-cylinder engines ever produced. Many owners report 250,000-300,000 mile lifespans with only basic maintenance.
Verify oil change history (5,000-7,500 mile intervals). Pull dipstick — oil should be golden to medium brown, not black or thick. Listen at idle for any tapping (valve adjustment may be needed at 100k+).
Est. cost: $200-$400 for valve adjustment if needed Source: Engine reliability data + owner reports

VTC actuator startup rattle (K24-specific)

Monitor
The K24Z3's Variable Timing Control (VTC) actuator can develop a sticky engagement, producing a distinct 1-3 second rattle on cold startup as the actuator engages and oil pressure builds. Honda issued an updated actuator design under TSB. Often misdiagnosed as timing chain noise by non-Honda shops.
Cold start the car after it has sat overnight or 6+ hours. Listen at the front of the engine for a distinct rattle that disappears within 1-3 seconds as oil pressure comes up. Persistent rattle = failed VTC actuator. NOT the timing chain.
Est. cost: $400-$700 VTC actuator replacement (NOT a $2,500 timing chain job) Source: Honda TSB + K-series engine community

Oil consumption (2009-2010 issue — verify NOT present on 2014)

Monitor
The 2009-2010 TSXes had documented oil consumption issues. By 2014, Honda had largely resolved this, but verify with the seller.
Ask: "Does the car burn oil between changes?" Pull the dipstick before and after a 10-minute drive. Check for blue smoke at startup or hard acceleration.
Est. cost: $2,500-$4,500 piston ring service if affected (rare on 2014) Source: Honda service campaign + TSB history

5-speed automatic transmission

Low Risk
The 5-speed automatic in the 2014 TSX is well-engineered when maintained. (Don't confuse with 2004-2005 first-gen transmission troubles — different unit entirely.)
Confirm transmission fluid has been changed at least once (Honda recommends 60k intervals using genuine Honda ATF DW-1 only — universal ATF causes shudder). Test all shift points during a 15-minute drive — smooth, no flares, no hard 1-2 shift.
Est. cost: $180 fluid service / $3,000+ rebuild if neglected Source: Honda service intervals + TSB history

6-speed manual clutch (if equipped)

Monitor
The 6-speed manual is sought-after and well-built, but a worn or abused clutch is expensive to replace. Many manual TSXes were enthusiast-owned and pushed hard. Third-gear synchro wear is a known weak point.
Engage 1st gear from a stop on a slight incline. Listen for chatter. Drive in 4th at 30 mph and gradually accelerate — if RPM rises faster than speed, clutch is slipping. Test 3rd gear engagement at low RPM (1500-2000) for any synchro grind.
Est. cost: $1,200-$1,800 clutch / $1,500+ synchro repair Source: TSX manual wear pattern

V6 knock sensor (P0325, 3.5L only)

Monitor
V6 TSXes commonly throw P0325 knock sensor codes by 100,000 miles. Inexpensive fix, but a frequent unexplained CEL trigger if the owner doesn't know.
Plug in an OBD2 scanner and check for stored or pending codes. Ask if the CEL has ever lit and what the diagnosis was. P0325 = knock sensor (cheap); P0420 = catalyst efficiency (expensive).
Est. cost: $200-$400 knock sensor + labor Source: V6 TSX forum-documented

Electrical

HID xenon ballast failure (Tech & V6 Tech)

Monitor
HID xenon ballasts on Tech and V6 Tech trims start failing by 100,000+ miles, causing flickering, dim, or completely dark headlights. OEM replacements are pricey but used-market parts are plentiful.
Cycle headlights on and off three times. Both sides should light immediately and at consistent brightness. Flicker, delayed startup, or color shift = ballast going.
Est. cost: $300-$500 OEM ballast new / $100-$200 used Source: TSX Tech trim forum pattern

Bose amplifier failure (Tech models)

Monitor
The Bose-amplified sound system in Technology trim has a known amplifier failure that causes cutting out, distortion, or one-channel-dead behavior. Often progressive over months.
Play music at moderate volume across multiple genres. Use balance/fader to verify each individual speaker plays clearly. Static, dropouts, or muffled speakers = amp going.
Est. cost: $400-$800 OEM amp / $200-$400 used Source: TSX Tech forum pattern

AcuraLink DVD navigation drive failure

Monitor
The Tech navigation system runs on a constantly-spinning DVD drive. The drive mechanism itself fails — not just the disc — rendering navigation unusable. Out-of-warranty replacement from Acura is very expensive.
Boot the car and confirm navigation loads within 60 seconds. Try plotting a route. Slow boot, freezing, or no response = the drive is failing or dead.
Est. cost: $400-$800 used unit / $1,800+ new from Acura Source: Acura forum-documented

Driver seat heated element burnout

Low Risk
The driver seat's heated element (Tech and V6 trims) fails earlier than passenger side because of more frequent use. Often a broken wire under the outer bolster.
Test heated seat on both driver and passenger at max setting for 5 minutes. Both should feel warm across the entire cushion and back. Cold spots = broken element.
Est. cost: $200-$400 element / $400+ labor (seat must come out) Source: Acura interior wear pattern

HVAC & Climate

A/C compressor clutch noise (Honda/Acura quirk)

Monitor
Common to Honda/Acura — light "tick" at cold start with A/C engaged is normal. Loud, constant cycling on/off, or persistent buzz = failing clutch or low refrigerant.
Cold start with A/C on max. Listen for engagement tick at engine bay. Constant on/off cycling within 30 seconds = low refrigerant (cheap fix) or failing clutch.
Est. cost: $600-$1,000 compressor / $80 refrigerant recharge Source: Honda/Acura compressor pattern

Climate control display dimming/burnout

Low Risk
The climate control panel display can develop dim segments or dead pixels by 100,000+ miles. Cosmetic but annoying.
Cycle through all HVAC modes and fan speeds. Verify display is fully readable in both bright (sunlight) and dim (night) conditions.
Est. cost: $200-$400 used replacement panel Source: Acura interior pattern

Steering & Suspension

EPS rack relearn after battery disconnect (mechanics often miss this)

Low Risk
Many shops do not know this: the TSX's electric power steering requires a steering angle sensor relearn after any battery disconnection or EPS-related repair. Without the relearn, the EPS may feel uneven or store a fault code that turns into a warning light weeks later.
Ask if any recent battery, alternator, or steering work has been done. If yes, the relearn procedure should have been performed — confirm with the shop. Test drive should feel even, consistent effort lock to lock with no warning lights.
Est. cost: $50-$120 if shop has to do relearn Source: Acura service procedure + commonly skipped

Power steering (2012 issue — verify NOT present on 2014)

Monitor
2012 TSXes had documented power steering rack issues. 2014 should be clean, but verify.
Check for any steering warning lights on the dash. Test steering feel at parking-lot speeds for unusual effort. Listen for whining or moaning when turning.
Est. cost: $1,500-$2,500 power steering rack if affected Source: TSB history

Front struts and lower control arm bushings

Monitor
At 10-12 years old, front struts and lower control arm bushings show wear, causing clunks over bumps and slightly degraded handling.
Drive over speed bumps and rough pavement. Listen for clunks. Push down hard on each corner — car should rebound once and settle, not bounce repeatedly.
Est. cost: $600-$1,000 struts (pair) / $200-$400 per bushing Source: Standard wear pattern

Body & Interior

Paint condition (2010-2014 thin clearcoat)

Low Risk
Acura's clearcoat from this era is thinner than the brand's premium reputation suggests. Minor stone chips and edge wear common.
Inspect hood, roof, and bumper edges in good light. Look for clearcoat haze, peeling, or repainted panels (mismatched orange-peel texture is the giveaway).
Est. cost: $400-$1,500 for spot repaint or detail Source: Common to Honda/Acura era

Rust (salt-belt states only)

Monitor
TSXes in salt-belt states can show rear quarter panel rust at the wheel arches by 10+ years.
Inspect rear wheel arches and rocker panels for rust bubbling. Use a magnet — won't stick to body-filler repair. Surface rust on undercarriage is normal; structural rust is not.
Est. cost: $800-$3,000+ for body repair Source: Salt-belt vehicle pattern

Sunroof drain tube clogging

Monitor
Sunroof drain channels at all four corners clog with debris over years, backing water up into the headliner or down A-pillars into the floor pan. Causes mold and electrical damage if undetected.
Open the sunroof and pour ~1 cup of water into the front drain channel. Water should exit at the fender behind the front wheels within 10 seconds. Pooling = clogged. Check carpet for damp spots.
Est. cost: $100-$300 cleaning / $$$ if interior damage exists Source: Common across all sunroof vehicles

Headlight projector lens haze

Low Risk
The plastic projector lens on TSX headlights has a clearcoat that fades and hazes by 100,000+ miles, dramatically reducing nighttime light output. Easy DIY restoration; full replacement is expensive on Tech trims (HID housing).
Inspect headlight lenses in daylight. Haziness, yellow tinge, or oxidation visible. Night drive will show poor illumination if affected.
Est. cost: $20-$60 DIY restoration kit / $500+ HID housing replacement Source: Plastic lens UV degradation pattern

Driver seat bolster wear (leather trims)

Low Risk
Standard leather wear. Driver outer bolster is first to show cracks or color loss.
Inspect driver seat outer-left bolster. Cracking is cosmetic but suggests the car has been driven heavily.
Est. cost: $200-$500 re-bolstering Source: Standard wear

Brakes & Wheels

Front brake judder (warped rotors)

Low Risk
TSX has adequate but not heroic front rotors. Hard-driven examples can warp by 50,000-70,000 miles, causing steering wheel shimmy under braking.
Brake firmly from 60 mph on a clear road. Steering wheel vibration during braking = warped rotors.
Est. cost: $350-$550 rotors + pads Source: TSX brake wear pattern

18" wheel cracking (Tech trims)

Low Risk
The optional 18" wheels on Tech trim are softer alloy than the 17"s and susceptible to crack damage from potholes.
Inspect inside wheel lip and around spoke roots for cracks. Steering pull or vibration at highway speed can indicate damage even when not visible.
Est. cost: $300-$600 per wheel repair / replacement Source: Tech trim wheel pattern

Non-desiccated inflator may rupture and spray metal fragments. 2009-2014 TSX. Free replacement.
19V-378
Incorrectly Installed Replacement Inflator
Inflators fitted under earlier Takata campaigns were installed wrong; the airbag may not deploy as designed. Honda campaigns M4O / P4R / T4Q / W4P.

Replacement Takata Inflator May Also Explode
Remedy and service-part inflators degrade the same way. Honda F5F / Q5E. A prior Takata repair does NOT close this one.

Open recalls — VIN check

What the VIN tells you

8th VIN digit engine code: ‘K’ = 2.4L K24Z3 I4 (the common choice — 5-speed AT or 6-speed manual). ‘J’ = 3.5L J35Z4 V6 (6-speed AT only). These engines have completely different inspection priorities: K24Z3 buyers must cold-start the car and listen for the VTC actuator startup rattle (a 1–3-second noise easily misdiagnosed as a timing chain issue — it is NOT) and should confirm the TSB-updated actuator design was applied. V6 buyers should scan for P0325 knock sensor codes (a cheap CEL trigger at 100k+) and verify 6-speed AT fluid history.
