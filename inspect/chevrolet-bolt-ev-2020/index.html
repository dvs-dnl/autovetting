Battery & Safety

Battery fire recall 22V-076 — critical

Critical
GM recalled certain Bolt EVs for a battery defect that caused fires in parked vehicles. Battery module replacement was the remedy. Verify this VIN's recall status before any purchase decision.
Run VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls BEFORE viewing the car. Recall 22V-076 must show "Remedy Available" and "Owner Notified" with dealer-completed work.
Est. cost: Free dealer battery module replacement under recall Source: NHTSA recall 22V-076

What the VIN tells you

On the 2020 Bolt EV the 8th VIN digit is not an engine code, and there is nothing anywhere in the VIN for it to fork. Measured by holding a constructed 2020 Bolt VIN constant and varying one position at a time through NHTSA vPIC: substituting any other character at position 8 returns vPIC error 14 — unable to provide information for some of the characters — rather than a different powertrain, because every U.S. 2020 Bolt EV is the same single front motor and the same 66 kWh pack. The position that does carry something is 5, and it carries trim: ‘Y’ = LT, ‘Z’ = Premier, each returned with the rest of the VIN unchanged and with electrification level still reading BEV. A control at position 6 left the trim at LT and the car electric, so position 5 is the trim character and not a neighbour effect. The practical consequence is that on a Bolt the VIN is not where the buying decision lives. The decode separates LT from Premier — an equipment difference, not a mechanical one — and nothing more. The question that decides the car is whether the battery-pack remedy for recall 22V-076 was actually performed on this specific VIN, and that is a lookup rather than an encoding: run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls, then ask the seller for the dealer paperwork showing what was replaced and when. A Bolt with a documented replacement pack is a different proposition from one still carrying its original cells with only the diagnostic software applied, and no VIN position will tell those two apart.
