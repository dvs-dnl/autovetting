2016 Mazda MX-5 Miata
Sport / Club / Grand Touring (ND, 4th gen, 2016-2023; RF hardtop from 2017)
Low Risk

The ND Mazda MX-5 Miata (2016-2023) is one of the most reliable and rewarding cheap sports cars you can buy used — light, simple, naturally aspirated, and free of the turbocharged or CVT failure modes that plague most affordable performance cars. The genuine risks are narrow: an open airbag-calibration recall (24V-695) on nearly every car, an automatic-transmission software recall (19V-072) on 2016-2019 autos, a soft top that leaks and tears if neglected, and the usual roadster wear items — clutch slave cylinder, rear-differential whine, thin paint. A manual ND2 with both recalls closed, a dry top, and clean service history is close to a no-risk buy.

Recalls & Safety

Airbag calibration recall 24V-695 (CRITICAL — nearly every ND)

Critical
NHTSA 24V-695 (Mazda, 2024) covers 77,670 model-year 2016-2023 MX-5 Miatas — almost the entire ND run. A misconfigured calibration tool left the Sophisticated Air Bag Sensor (SAS) module able to deploy the front airbags with too much force, outside FMVSS 208 limits. The remedy is a free dealer software reflash of the SAS module.
Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov and confirm 24V-695 shows as completed. This recall is so broad that most used cars still need it. The fix is free and fast, but an open airbag recall is a hard bargaining point and a safety item — never skip it.
Est. cost: $0 (free dealer reflash) — leverage it on price if open Source: NHTSA recall 24V-695 (2024) — 2016-2023 Mazda MX-5 Miata, 77,670 units

Automatic-transmission TCM recall 19V-072 (2016-2019 autos only)

High Attention
NHTSA 19V-072 (Mazda campaign 3019A, Feb 2019) covers about 14,370 2016-2019 MX-5s with the 6-speed automatic. Electrical noise in the range signal could trigger faulty clutch-control logic in the TCM and cause an unexpected downshift and abrupt deceleration of the drive wheels. Remedy is a free TCM reprogram. Manual cars are not affected.
If the car is an automatic, confirm 19V-072 is closed at nhtsa.gov. On the test drive, note any unexpected harsh downshift or sudden deceleration at steady throttle. Manual-transmission cars can skip this item entirely.
Est. cost: $0 (free TCM reprogram) Source: NHTSA recall 19V-072 / Mazda 3019A (Feb 2019) — 2016-2019 MX-5 automatic

Soft Top & Body

Soft-top leaks, tears, and water intrusion

High Attention
The ND cloth top is light and quick but the single most common ND complaint. Seams and the area around the rear window crack and leak with age and sun; water pools on the seats and can reach the door-speaker wiring and interior electronics. A neglected or torn top is the costliest single ND repair.
Inspect the seams, the rear-window surround, and the header seal for cracks, tears, or stiffness; check after rain for water on the seats and footwells. Open and close the top several times — it should latch one-handed with no binding. Lift the carpet edges to feel for dampness or a musty smell.
Est. cost: $700-$2,000 soft-top replacement (more for a power or RF unit) Source: ND owner complaint patterns; TopMiata / MX-5 owner forums

RF retractable hardtop operation (2017+ RF only)

Monitor
The RF (Retractable Fastback, 2017 on) trades the cloth top for a powered hardtop with flying buttresses. The mechanism is generally reliable but complex; a failed motor or sensor can strand the roof partway. RF cars also weigh more and are not full convertibles — the buttresses stay up.
On an RF, cycle the roof fully open and closed twice (it only operates below about 6 mph). Listen for grinding or hesitation and confirm it seats and latches cleanly both ways. Any partial-cycle fault means an expensive mechanism diagnosis.
Est. cost: $1,500-$4,000+ RF mechanism repair if the motor or linkage fails Source: Mazda RF service documentation; ND RF owner reports

Thin paint and stone chips

Low Risk
Mazda water-based paint is thin, and Soul Red in particular shows chips, swirls, and clearcoat wear early. A minor color or texture mismatch between plastic and metal panels is normal from the factory and is not evidence of a repaint. This is cosmetic, not structural.
Inspect the nose, sills, and front of the arches for chips, and the whole car under angled light for swirl marks. Mismatched panel gaps or overspray (not slight color difference) would suggest prior accident repair — verify with a paint-depth gauge if in doubt.
Est. cost: $50-$150 touch-up; $400-$800 per-panel respray if desired Source: ND paint documentation; owner reports

Engine — 2.0L SkyActiv-G PE-VPS

Confirm ND1 vs ND2 (155 hp vs 181 hp)

Monitor
2016-2018 cars (ND1) make 155 hp and rev to about 6,800 rpm. The 2019 refresh (ND2) revised the same 2.0L to 181 hp, added a 7,500-rpm redline and a dual-mass flywheel, and improved throttle response. Both are durable, but an ND2 drives noticeably better and commands a price premium — make sure you are paying for the version you are getting.
Confirm the model year from the 10th VIN digit and verify the tach redline (6,800 vs 7,500). A 2019-or-newer car should rev past 7,000 freely. Do not pay ND2 money for an ND1.
Est. cost: n/a — valuation item Source: Mazda ND specifications; NHTSA VIN model-year coding

Oil level, condition, and minor oil leaks

Monitor
The PE-VPS is a robust naturally aspirated engine with no chronic oil-consumption problem, but ND owners report minor seepage from valve-cover and timing-cover gaskets at higher mileage. Track-driven cars benefit from a baffled oil pan, so heavy track history warrants extra scrutiny.
Check the dipstick for level and clean amber oil, and inspect the front and top of the engine for oil weep. Ask for oil-change records (5W-30, about 7,500-mile intervals). Look for track stickers, harness bars, or worn pedals that hint at hard use.
Est. cost: $40-$70 oil change; $300-$700 valve-cover/timing-cover gasket if leaking Source: PE-VPS field reports; ND owner forums

i-stop / i-ELOOP and cold-start behavior

Low Risk
ND Miatas use Mazda i-stop auto start/stop (and i-ELOOP regenerative capacitor on some trims), which needs a specific high-spec battery; a cheap replacement battery disables i-stop and can throw warnings. Early-build cars (2015-2017) had an isolated i-ELOOP overheating concern addressed in some markets.
Confirm i-stop engages at a warm idle stop and the engine restarts instantly and smoothly. A disabled i-stop usually means a non-spec battery was fitted. Cold-start should be clean with no persistent rattle.
Est. cost: $200-$300 correct-spec battery if i-stop is disabled Source: Mazda i-stop / i-ELOOP documentation

Drivetrain & Chassis

Clutch slave cylinder and manual shift feel

High Attention
On manual cars the clutch slave cylinder can leak with age, giving a soft clutch pedal that sinks to the floor or makes engagement vague. The ND 6-speed itself is excellent and notchy when healthy; grinding into 2nd or a long soft pedal points to slave-cylinder or synchro wear.
Pump the clutch and feel for a firm, consistent bite point; shift through all six gears, especially 2nd, listening for grind. A pedal that sinks or a clutch that slips under hard 3rd-gear acceleration needs further inspection. Check under the car for fluid at the bellhousing.
Est. cost: $200-$500 clutch slave cylinder; $1,200-$2,000 clutch job if worn Source: ND drivetrain complaint patterns; MX-5 owner forums

Rear-differential whine and fluid

Monitor
A whine from the rear differential under acceleration or steady cruise can indicate worn gears or low/old fluid; faint axle noise on deceleration is normal on the ND and can be ignored. Hard-launched or track cars wear the diff and its mounts faster.
At 40-60 mph, lift and reapply throttle and listen for a rising-and-falling whine from behind you. A steady whine that tracks engine load warrants a diff inspection. Ask whether the diff fluid has ever been changed.
Est. cost: $80-$150 diff fluid; $1,000-$2,500 differential rebuild if worn Source: ND owner forums; MX-5 differential service reports

Suspension hardware corrosion (salt-belt cars)

Monitor
ND owners in salt-belt states report rusting suspension bolts and bushing hardware, which complicates future alignments and suspension work even though the unibody itself holds up well. A garage-kept southern car is markedly cleaner underneath.
Inspect subframe bolts, control-arm hardware, and the exhaust for surface rust and seized fasteners. Heavy rust on the hardware is a labor multiplier on any future suspension job. Compare against the claimed region and storage.
Est. cost: $0 to inspect; adds $200-$600 labor to future suspension work if seized Source: ND owner reports; salt-belt corrosion patterns

Electrical & Interior

Mazda Connect (CMU) infotainment glitches

Low Risk
The ND Mazda Connect commander-dial system is reliable but can suffer unresponsive screens, freezes, or connectivity drops; many are resolved by a CMU firmware update at the dealer. 2016-2017 cars lacked factory Apple CarPlay/Android Auto, which Mazda later offered as a retrofit.
Power-cycle the screen, test Bluetooth pairing, the backup camera, and (if equipped) CarPlay. Confirm the screen responds to both touch (when parked) and the commander dial. A frozen or rebooting CMU usually just needs a firmware update.
Est. cost: $0-$200 CMU firmware update; about $500 CarPlay retrofit on 2016-2017 Source: Mazda Connect documentation; ND owner reports

Rear glass and defroster (soft-top cars)

Low Risk
The cloth top rear window is heated glass on the ND (an improvement over older clear-vinyl tops), but the surrounding fabric and the defroster element can fail. Scratches or hazing on the glass and a dead defroster are common with age and careless folding.
Inspect the rear glass for scratches and haze, and test the rear defroster. Confirm the top folds without forcing the glass. Damage here usually accompanies a tired top overall.
Est. cost: $150-$500 rear-window/defroster repair if the glass or element fails Source: ND soft-top documentation; owner reports

Battery health and parasitic drain

Low Risk
As a light, often second-car roadster, ND Miatas are frequently driven infrequently, which kills batteries; the i-stop system also demands a healthy high-spec battery. A weak battery can mimic electrical gremlins such as CMU resets and warning lights.
Note the battery date and have it load-tested; confirm it is the correct i-stop spec. If the car sat unused, a fresh battery often clears intermittent electrical faults. Confirm no warning lights remain after a proper start.
Est. cost: $200-$300 correct-spec battery Source: ND ownership patterns; Mazda i-stop battery spec

What the VIN tells you

Every U.S. ND Miata uses the same 2.0L SkyActiv-G PE-VPS four-cylinder, so there is no engine-family ambiguity to decode — the distinction that matters is ND1 vs ND2. Use the 10th VIN digit (model year) to separate them: 2016-2018 cars (ND1) make 155 hp and rev to about 6,800; 2019-2020 cars (ND2) make 181 hp, add a dual-mass flywheel, and rev to 7,500. An ND2 is the more desirable engine and is worth a price premium. Always run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov before purchase to confirm airbag recall 24V-695 (all 2016-2023) and, on automatic cars, TCM recall 19V-072 (2016-2019) are closed.
