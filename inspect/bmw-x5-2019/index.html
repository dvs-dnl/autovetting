What the VIN tells you

On the G05 X5 the 8th VIN digit is not the engine character, which is the most useful thing to know before decoding one — verified against NHTSA vPIC by changing that one character and nothing else off 5UXCR6C*1KLL00000: ‘5’ and ‘3’ both return X5 / xDrive40i / 3.0L / 6 cylinders / 335 hp, identical in every engine field, while ‘0’ and ‘7’ return decode errors. The engine lives in positions 4–7, the model-designation block: ‘CR6C’ returns xDrive40i, 3.0L, 6 cylinders, 335 hp (the B58 inline-six) and ‘JU2C’ returns xDrive50i, 4.4L, 8 cylinders, 456 hp (the N63 V8). That fork is the entire risk story on this truck — the N63 items below are the difference between budgeted BMW running costs and a $6,000–$12,000 engine bill — and a tailgate badge is the easiest thing on the car to change, so read positions 4–7 off the actual VIN rather than the listing. One more pattern to recognize: ‘KR0C’ with a 2019 year character decodes clean as X5 xDrive35i, 3.0L, 300 hp, which is the previous-generation F15 pattern — if a decode returns xDrive35i or 300 hp you are not looking at a G05, and this checklist’s air-suspension and B58 water-pump items do not map. And per house rule: a decode returning a configuration is evidence of what the VIN encodes, not proof that configuration was sold in that market and year. Use it to identify the car in front of you, not to argue availability.
