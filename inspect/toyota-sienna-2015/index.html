What the VIN tells you

Toyota encodes the engine in the 5th VIN digit, not the 8th. On a 2015 Sienna it always reads ‘K’ — the 3.5L 2GR-FE V6 (266 hp / 245 lb-ft) — because 2015 has no engine choice at all: the 2.7L four was gone by 2013, and the 296-hp 2GR-FKS with the 8-speed automatic does not arrive until 2017. So the engine character tells you nothing here, and that is exactly the point — what you need out of this VIN is front-wheel drive versus AWD, which changes the inspection far more than any engine option would. Decode the full VIN at autovetting.com/decode and confirm the drive type before you go look at the van. An AWD 2015 Sienna has no spare tire: the rear driveshaft and exhaust occupy the spare well, and Toyota compensated with P235/55RF18 run-flats. Budget for that — OE-size run-flats run roughly $200–$250 apiece against about $130–$160 for a conventional tire in the same size, so a set costs several hundred dollars more, and a flat on a van with no spare is a tow. Check the date codes on all four, and check whether a previous owner quietly switched to conventional tires: that is not a defect, but it means the van now has neither a run-flat reserve nor a spare. AWD also adds a driveshaft, rear differential and transfer case a front-drive Sienna simply does not have — ask for rear-diff fluid history on any AWD car past 100k.
