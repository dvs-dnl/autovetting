What the VIN tells you

On the BT Outback the engine is encoded at position 6, not position 8, so reading the 8th digit will tell you nothing about which engine you are buying. Measured by holding a constructed 2020 Outback VIN constant and varying one position at a time through NHTSA vPIC: at position 6, ‘A’ returns the 2.5L four at 182 hp (vPIC engine model ‘U5’) while ‘G’ and ‘H’ both return the 2.4L turbo at 260 hp (vPIC engine model ‘U4’); other characters there return vPIC error 14. Three controls separate position 6 from its neighbours: changing position 8 left the engine at 2.5L/182 hp, changing position 7 left it at 2.5L, and changing position 9 — the check digit — left it at 2.5L as well. So position 6 is the fork, and it is the digit that decides your inspection. Read ‘A’ and you are inspecting the naturally-aspirated FB25D: oil consumption from roughly 80k miles, plus CVT fluid history. Read ‘G’ or ‘H’ and you are inspecting the FA24F XT, which keeps the CVT question under more torque and adds turbo-specific upkeep — the shorter synthetic oil interval, evidence it was actually kept to, and boost-system and coolant-hose condition. The 260 hp car is the one where a ‘just serviced’ claim needs records rather than a verbal. One caution on reading vPIC output: it does not validate combinations, so a VIN that pairs the 2.4L turbo with a trim Subaru never sold it in will still decode and still report that trim. Treat the engine character as the reliable part and confirm trim from the window sticker or build sheet. Run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov to confirm every open campaign is closed.
