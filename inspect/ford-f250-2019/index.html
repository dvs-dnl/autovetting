The Super Duty F-250 is the workhorse of the truck segment — built for heavy towing and payload. The 6.7L Power Stroke diesel is the towing king but requires DEF and has injector/turbo maintenance. The 6.2L/7.3L gasoline V8s are simpler. Main buyer risks: diesel injector failure at high mileage, turbo oil supply tube (known diesel issue), transmission wear from heavy towing, and cab/frame corrosion on work trucks.

Diesel Engine (6.7L Power Stroke)

Fuel injector wear at high mileage

High Risk at 150k+ diesel
Power Stroke injectors are expensive and wear on heavy-use trucks. A full set replacement is $3,000–$5,000.
Test cold-start idle quality. Any rough running, smoke, or misfires = request injector test.
Est. cost: $3,000–$5,000 full injector set Source: Ford Power Stroke maintenance pattern

Turbocharger oil supply tube (CP4 related)

Monitor
The CP4.2 high-pressure fuel pump had metal contamination issues on early 6.7L builds. Later builds improved.
Ask for fuel system service history. Any HPFP replacement documented = healthy sign the issue was addressed.
Est. cost: $2,000–$8,000 if HPFP fails Source: Ford 6.7L CP4 pattern

DEF system maintenance

Low Risk
Diesel Exhaust Fluid top-up every 5k miles and SCR catalyst service.
Check DEF level and confirm no emissions fault codes.
Est. cost: $50/fill-up + periodic SCR service Source: Ford DEF standard

Transmission wear from towing

Monitor
Super Duties used for max-payload towing experience accelerated transmission wear. Check fluid color and records.
Request transmission fluid service records. Dark fluid with particles = worn.
Est. cost: $300–$500 fluid service; $4,000+ rebuild Source: AutoVetting editorial

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

20V-576
Front axle wheel-end yoke may not be welded to the axle tube (2019 F-250/F-350 4x4)
On 4x4 trucks the front axle wheel-end yoke may not be properly welded to the axle tube, and a failure there costs you control of the truck. Scope is the 2019 F-250 and F-350 4x4 in both single and dual rear wheel configurations, so this is a this-truck campaign and not a Super-Duty-wide one. Remedy: free dealer inspection of the weld location, with the axle assembly replaced if the weld is misplaced.
19V-688
Rear axle shaft can fracture on electronic-locking-differential trucks (2019 F-250/F-350)
The passenger-side rear axle shaft can fail prematurely from a material defect on trucks built with the electronically locking rear differential. A broken shaft means loss of drive power and the truck cannot be held in Park, so if the parking brake is not set it can roll away. Remedy: free dealer inspection of the passenger-side shaft, replaced as needed.
18V-894
Engine block heater cable can short and overheat wiring (2017-2019 Super Duty)
Water and road contaminants corrode the block-heater cable splice connector; prolonged corrosion causes a resistive short that overheats or melts the wiring and raises fire risk. It matters most on northern and work trucks that actually plugged the heater in. Remedy: dealers first disabled the block heater cable and later replaced it, free of charge, so a truck whose block heater simply does not work may be parked at the interim fix rather than the final one.
19V-633
Front seat recliner may be missing a pawl (2019-2020 Super Duty, manual recliners)
Manual seat-back recliners can be missing a third pawl, which leaves the seat back loose and out of compliance with the federal seating standard, so it may not adequately restrain an occupant in a crash. Remedy: free dealer inspection of the seat structure with replacement as needed. Worth checking by hand on a truck whose seat back feels loose, since the defect is physical and present from new.
