What the VIN tells you

On the 2nd-gen Envision the 8th VIN digit is not an engine code, and reading it as one will mislead you. Measured by holding a constructed 2021 Envision VIN constant and varying one position at a time through NHTSA vPIC: substituting any other character at position 8 returns vPIC error 14 — unable to provide information for some of the characters — rather than a different powertrain. There is nothing there to fork, because every U.S. 2021 Envision is the same single turbocharged 2.0L four paired with the 9-speed automatic. The position that actually forks this car is 6, and it carries the drive layout: ‘R’ = front-wheel drive, ‘S’ = all-wheel drive, confirmed by changing only that character and watching vPIC return FWD and then AWD with the rest of the VIN unchanged. That is the digit to read, because it changes part of your inspection and not just the badge: an AWD Envision adds a rear drive unit and a clutch-pack coupling with their own fluid-service history to ask about, and it is the version that can produce driveline shudder or a rear-end hum that a front-drive car cannot. Engine-side the inspection is the same whichever way position 6 reads — this 2.0L turbo has a documented oil-consumption pattern, so check the level against the date of the last service, look for oil residue at the turbo feed, and treat a car with no oil-change records as an unknown rather than as a clean one. Run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov to confirm every open campaign is closed.
