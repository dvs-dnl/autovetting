Power Train

Powertrain

VVT-i OIL HOSE — CRITICAL on 2010 (engine killer if not recalled)

Critical Risk
Early 2010 RX 350s — and many 2007-2010 Toyota/Lexus 2GR-FE vehicles — used a RUBBER VVT-i oil line that can rupture without warning, causing rapid total oil loss and catastrophic engine failure within minutes. Toyota issued LSC 90G (a limited service campaign, not a full recall) to replace the rubber line with a steel-braided one. Many cars never got it.
This is the single most important check on a 2010 RX 350. Open the hood and locate the VVT-i oil line at the front of the engine — a steel braided sleeve covering the line means the recall was performed. Bare rubber hose with crimped fittings means it WAS NOT. Verify completion via VIN lookup at lexus.com/recalls or NHTSA.gov/recalls. If unrecalled, do not buy until the work is done.
Est. cost: Free if recall performed (LSC 90G) / $8,000+ engine replacement if it ruptures Source: Toyota Limited Service Campaign 90G + widely documented engine failures

2GR-FE V6 — the "unkillable" engine

No Known Issues
The 3.5L 2GR-FE is one of the most reliable V6s Toyota ever built. With basic oil-change maintenance, 250,000-300,000 miles is normal.
Verify oil change history. Pull dipstick: oil should be amber to brown, not black. Check for any oil leaks at the front and rear of the engine. Listen for unusual rattles at startup.
Est. cost: $0 if maintained / $400 valve service if rare ticking Source: Toyota 2GR-FE reliability data

Water pump (preventive replacement zone)

High Attention
The 2GR-FE water pump typically needs replacement between 90,000 and 130,000 miles. If overdue, this is the single most important non-recall thing to address before purchase.
Look for coolant weeping at the front of the engine. Ask the seller if the water pump has been replaced (request the invoice). Check coolant level and color in reservoir.
Est. cost: $600-$900 (parts + labor at independent shop) Source: Toyota service interval + common failure

VVT-i actuator startup rattle (separate from oil line)

Monitor
Similar to other 2GR-FE applications, the VVT-i actuator can develop a brief startup rattle as it engages. NOT to be confused with the critical oil-line item above — this is a much smaller issue.
Cold start the car after sitting 6+ hours. Listen for 1-3 second rattle from front of engine that disappears as oil pressure builds. Persistent rattle = actuator going.
Est. cost: $400-$700 actuator if persistent Source: Toyota 2GR-FE common pattern

Timing chain (NOT a belt — confirms no $1,000+ scheduled service)

Low Risk
The 2GR-FE uses a timing chain, not a belt. No scheduled replacement is required. Confirm with the seller — some buyers and even some shops mistakenly think this engine has a belt and pay for unnecessary "service."
Listen for any chain rattle at cold start. If a few seconds of rattle before oil pressure builds, the chain tensioner may be weakening (uncommon).
Est. cost: $1,800-$2,500 if tensioner replacement needed (rare) Source: Toyota 2GR-FE engine specs

6-speed automatic transmission

Low Risk
Reliable when fluid is changed periodically. Some 2010-2011 units developed minor lurching on the 1-2 shift when fluid was neglected (24 NHTSA reports — fluid service usually resolves).
Confirm transmission fluid has been changed at least once (60k interval recommended, Toyota WS fluid only). Test all shift points during a 20-minute drive — smooth, no flares, no harsh 1-2 shift.
Est. cost: $250 fluid service / $3,500-$5,000 rebuild Source: Toyota service intervals + NHTSA reports

Brakes & Wheels

Brake actuator / master cylinder failure (2010-12 specific)

High Attention
Some 2010-12 RX 350s have a documented brake actuator (master cylinder assembly) failure that triggers brake warnings and a stiff pedal. Toyota issued related campaigns. Generic shops often misdiagnose as ABS.
Brake feel should be firm and consistent from light to firm application. Pedal going slowly to floor, or any brake warning lights, indicates the assembly is failing. Verify any related recalls completed via VIN.
Est. cost: $1,200-$2,000 actuator replacement (if not under recall) Source: NHTSA recall + owner reports

Front brake judder (heavy SUV on stock rotors)

Monitor
The RX 350's significant curb weight causes stock rotors to warp by 60,000-80,000 miles, especially if used for occasional towing or aggressive driving.
Brake firmly from 60 mph on a clear stretch. Steering wheel shimmy = warped rotors. Inspect rotor surface visually through wheel spokes.
Est. cost: $500-$800 rotors + pads (use premium parts) Source: RX 350 brake wear pattern

Suspension & Steering

Front struts and rear shocks (age-related)

Monitor
At typical RX 350 age (14-16 years now), front struts and rear shocks are due for replacement, causing a floaty or "boaty" ride.
Push down hard on each corner. Car should rebound once and settle, not bounce repeatedly. Listen for clunks over bumps. Ride should feel composed.
Est. cost: $800-$1,400 struts (pair) / $400-$700 rear shocks Source: Standard wear pattern

Rack-and-pinion minor seal leaks

Low Risk
Some 2010-2011 units develop slow rack seal leaks. Not urgent unless the fluid level drops noticeably.
Check power steering fluid level (engine off, cold). Inspect ground beneath car after parked overnight for fluid spots. Listen for whining when turning.
Est. cost: $1,200-$1,800 rack replacement if leak is significant Source: Owner reports

Electrical

Remote Touch joystick controller (2010-12 only)

Monitor
The 2010-12 Lexus Remote Touch joystick-style controller (replaced by a trackpad in 2013+) develops sticky directional input or completely fails. Common 100k+ wear point.
Cycle through every nav and audio menu using the joystick. Test all four directions and the click action. Sticky direction = failing controller.
Est. cost: $400 used / $1,500+ new from Lexus Source: Lexus forum-documented

Ventilated seat fan failure

Low Risk
On equipped trims, the small DC fans under the seat cushion that drive cooled-seat function fail with use, usually in one seat first.
Activate cooled seat function for both driver and passenger. Cool air should be felt through perforations within 30 seconds. Listen for fan noise — silence with cooling active = dead fan.
Est. cost: $200-$400 per fan (parts; labor varies) Source: Lexus interior wear pattern

Mark Levinson amplifier (premium audio trims)

Monitor
On ML-equipped cars, the trunk-mounted subwoofer amplifier can develop dropouts or fail entirely. Common high-mileage issue.
Play bass-heavy music at moderate volume. Listen for dropouts, distortion, or audible "click" sounds. Use balance/fader to verify each speaker plays cleanly.
Est. cost: $300-$600 used amp / $1,200+ new Source: Mark Levinson amp pattern

HVAC & Climate

HVAC blend door actuator clicking

Monitor
Behind-dash blend door actuators develop a repetitive clicking sound at 80,000+ miles. Common across Toyota/Lexus of this era. Multiple actuators in the dash.
Cycle all HVAC mode buttons (face, foot, defrost). Listen for repeated clicking from behind dash. Verify air actually changes direction with each mode.
Est. cost: $300-$500 per actuator Source: Toyota/Lexus HVAC pattern

Rear climate panel (3-zone climate trims)

Low Risk
The rear-passenger climate panel (3-zone climate trims) can fail to respond, leaving rear at front's temperature setting.
Adjust rear climate from rear panel. Verify temperature and fan respond independently from front.
Est. cost: $200-$400 panel Source: Lexus 3-zone HVAC pattern

Body & Interior

Dashboard leather peeling (Phoenix-area cars especially)

High Attention
2010-13 RX 350s have a notorious dashboard top leather peeling/cracking issue, particularly in hot climates like Arizona and Texas. Lexus extended the warranty for this in some regions; many owners had dashes replaced free under TSB. Check Lexus dealer for warranty coverage before paying out of pocket.
Inspect the top of the dashboard near the windshield and HVAC vents in good light. Cracking, bubbling, peeling, or sticky tackiness = the known defect. Phoenix-area cars are especially affected.
Est. cost: $0-$2,500 (free under extended warranty / $1,500-$2,500 if out of warranty) Source: Lexus TSB + Phoenix/Texas market pattern

Sunroof drain channels (clog risk)

Monitor
Sunroof drain tubes can clog with debris over time, causing water leaks into the headliner or floor pan.
Open the sunroof and pour ~1 cup of water into the front drain channel. Water should exit at the fender area within 10 seconds. Pooling = clogged drains. Check headliner and carpet for water stains or damp spots.
Est. cost: $100-$250 drain cleaning (DIY: free) Source: Common across all sunroof-equipped vehicles

Sliding sun visor breakage

Low Risk
The sun visor's sliding extension mechanism is plastic and notoriously fragile on the RX 350. Common to find at least one broken visor.
Test both visors fully — pull down, swing to side, extend the slider. Cracks, wobble, or refusal to extend = broken.
Est. cost: $150-$300 per visor Source: RX 350 known issue

Power liftgate motor and struts

Low Risk
On power-liftgate equipped trims, the motor and gas struts weaken with use, causing the liftgate to fall or refuse to lift.
Operate the power liftgate three times. Should open smoothly to full height. With gate half-open and motor off, gas struts should hold position; if it slowly falls, struts are weak.
Est. cost: $150-$300 gas struts / $600+ motor Source: Standard wear pattern

Fuel filler door release jam

Low Risk
The internal fuel door release mechanism can stick or fail, leaving you unable to open the fuel door at the pump.
Press the fuel door release inside the car. Door should pop open immediately. Sticky or unresponsive = plan for replacement.
Est. cost: $100-$250 mechanism + labor Source: RX 350 known issue

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

LSC 90G
VVT-i Oil Hose (rubber to steel) — 2010 builds
CRITICAL — a Toyota Limited Service Campaign, not an NHTSA recall. The rubber VVT-i oil line can rupture and destroy the engine. Verify completion by VIN at lexus.com/recalls before buying a 2010 car.
11V-377
Brake Actuator — VDIM Calibration
The only NHTSA safety recall listed for the 2011 RX 350. Cars built Aug 30, 2010 through May 18, 2011 with Vehicle Dynamics Integrated Management received hydraulic control valves with incorrect calibration, so the right front wheel may not be controlled properly when ABS or VSC activates — longer stopping distance or a failure to correct a slide. Dealers replace the brake actuator free.

Open recalls — VIN check
The 2010 and 2012 model years carry their own campaign lists. Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and require every open recall closed before purchase.

What the VIN tells you

Unlike most vehicles in this suite, the 2010-2012 RX 350 offers only one engine — the 3.5L 2GR-FE V6. The 8th VIN digit confirms the restraint system only; there is no engine fork to decode. The most buyer-critical VIN position is 10 (the model year digit): A = 2010, B = 2011, C = 2012. This matters because 2010 cars (position 10 = A) require mandatory verification of Toyota Limited Service Campaign LSC 90G — the rubber VVT-i oil hose recall. If you are looking at a 2010 (position 10 = A) and cannot confirm LSC 90G was performed via VIN lookup at lexus.com/recalls or NHTSA, walk away. 2011 (B) and 2012 (C) cars are statistically safer starting points because the campaign had been widely completed by then. AWD vs. FWD is the other key decode: run the VIN through NHTSA vPIC (vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov) to confirm drivetrain — AWD is the common choice and commands a $1,500-$3,000 premium on the used market.
