2019 Nissan Altima
2.5 S / SV / SL / SR / Platinum — also AWD (2.5 only) (L34, 6th gen, 2019–2023)
Monitor

The 6th-gen Altima (L34, 2019–2023) is a meaningful improvement over the notoriously fragile L33 generation. The 2.5L PR25DD is a more robust engine than the old QR25DE, and AWD is finally available. Two risks remain: (1) the 2.0L VC-Turbo (SR/Platinum FWD) burns oil and had an engine-bearing recall — verify the ECM update is done; and (2) the CVT8 is better than the old JF015E but still shudders on neglected fluid. A 2.5L AWD model with documented CVT fluid history and a completed camera recall is the safest pick.

CVT Transmission

CVT8 shudder and hesitation (gen-6 improvement, still present)

High Attention
The L34 uses the Jatco CVT8 (JF017E), a stronger unit than the L33 JF015E. NHTSA CVT complaints are far below L33 levels, but VC-Turbo heat and neglected fluid still cause early shudder and hesitation. Replacement is $2,800–$4,500.
Accelerate briskly from 20 to 60 mph then hold 45 mph at light throttle for 30 seconds. Any shudder, belt-slip, or power interruption is a red flag. Ask for documented CVT fluid service records — NS-3 fluid, every 30,000 miles under severe conditions.
Est. cost: $2,800–$4,500 CVT replacement (remanufactured JF017E) Source: NHTSA complaint database 2019–2023 Altima; Nissan service manual NS-3 specification

CVT fluid color and service history

High Attention
Nissan NS-3 fluid is red/pink when fresh. Dark brown fluid with a burnt smell has never been changed. Degraded fluid dramatically accelerates belt and pulley wear inside the CVT — this is the single most preventable failure mode.
Ask for the CVT fluid service date and confirm NS-3 fluid was used. If history is unknown, budget $150–$200 for immediate service before driving the car long-distance.
Est. cost: $150–$200 CVT fluid service Source: Nissan L34 service manual; CVT fluid degradation data

Engine — VC-Turbo and 2.5L PR25DD

VC-Turbo oil consumption and bearing recall 25V437 (CRITICAL if equipped)

Critical
The 2.0L KR20DDET variable-compression turbo is only on SR/Platinum FWD models. NHTSA 25V437 (June 2025) covers 2019–2020 VC-Turbo Altimas for engine-bearing manufacturing defects that can lead to catastrophic engine failure — fix is an ECM reprogram. Separately, VC-Turbo engines are known to consume 1 quart per 1,500–3,000 miles on some units.
Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov before purchasing — confirm recall 25V437 is completed on any 2019–2020 VC-Turbo car. Check the oil level cold; below MIN or milky/foamy oil signals internal damage. Ask for oil-level check records and any dealer recall documentation.
Est. cost: $7,000–$12,000 engine replacement if bearing failure has occurred Source: NHTSA recall 25V437 (June 2025) — 2019–2020 Nissan Altima VC-Turbo

VC-Turbo shaft and turbocharger condition (if equipped)

Monitor
The KR20DDET turbocharger relies on consistent oil pressure. Low-oil-level events accelerate turbo bearing wear. Listen for a high-pitched whine from the engine bay under light acceleration at 1,500–2,500 rpm.
During the test drive, listen for turbo whine or grinding from the driver-side engine bay under acceleration. A healthy turbo is nearly inaudible. Any metallic noise = further inspection.
Est. cost: $1,800–$3,500 turbocharger replacement Source: Nissan technician service reports; AltimaTalk owner forums

2.5L PR25DD oil level and engine condition (base/SV/SL/AWD)

Monitor
The naturally aspirated 2.5L PR25DD is the safer, more durable choice. Not prone to excessive oil consumption. Verify oil is at level and clean. The timing chain is generally long-lived — a brief rattle on cold start at very high mileage (120,000+) warrants inspection but is rare.
Check dipstick: oil should be between MIN and MAX, amber to light brown, no grit or burnt smell. Start the engine cold and listen for the first 10 seconds; a persistent chain rattle after that warrants a timing-chain inspection.
Est. cost: $40–$70 oil change; $800–$1,500 timing chain service if needed Source: PR25DD field service data; L34 Altima owner reports

ADAS and Rear Camera

Rear camera recall 23V628 / R23C3 (2019–2021 Platinum)

High Attention
NHTSA recall 23V628 (Nissan campaign R23C3, September 2023) affects 2019–2021 Altima Platinum models. The rear camera wiring harness can chafe and produce a blurry, distorted, or multi-colored backup image — a direct safety hazard. Fix is harness securing or camera replacement at no charge.
Put the car in Reverse and confirm the backup camera shows a clear, undistorted image. Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov to confirm R23C3 is completed on Platinum-trim cars.
Est. cost: Covered under recall (no cost to owner) Source: NHTSA recall 23V628 / Nissan campaign R23C3 — September 11, 2023

ProPilot Assist camera calibration (SV and above)

Monitor
ProPilot Assist (available on SV trim and above from 2019) uses a windshield-mounted forward camera for adaptive cruise and lane-keep assist. Camera misalignment after windshield replacement or minor collision repair causes repeated "ProPilot Assist unavailable" alerts or failed lane-keep.
Enable ProPilot Assist during a highway test drive — the system should hold lane and maintain gap without repeated dropouts. Multiple alerts = camera recalibration needed ($200–$400).
Est. cost: $200–$400 ProPilot Assist camera recalibration Source: Nissan ProPilot Assist service manual; owner reports

Brakes, Suspension, and AWD

Brake rotor condition and pulsation check

Monitor
Front rotors typically last 40,000–60,000 miles. Pulsation during braking (steering wheel shimmy at 50–70 mph) = warped rotors. Single-piston front calipers are inexpensive to service.
Brake firmly from 50 mph — pedal should be firm and linear, no pulsation. Inspect rotor edges through wheel spokes for deep grooves.
Est. cost: $300–$550 front brake service (pads and rotors) Source: Standard brake inspection protocol

Sway bar links and front strut condition

Monitor
L34 Altimas develop a clunk over bumps from worn sway bar end links at 50,000–80,000 miles. Front struts are generally long-lived but check for shimmy above 65 mph and excessive dive under braking.
At low speed, turn lock-to-lock and listen for clunking from the front end. At highway speed, note any vibration or wander. Bounce the front corners — one oscillation and stop is normal.
Est. cost: $120–$280 sway bar links; $500–$900 front struts per side Source: L34 Altima suspension service data; AltimaTalk forum

AWD coupling function (AWD models only)

Monitor
AWD is only available on 2.5L models. The electronically controlled rear coupling should engage smoothly. Shudder under AWD load (turning tightly with the system active) indicates coupling wear or fluid degradation.
On a gravel or loose surface, make a tight full-lock circle — no shudder or binding should occur. If not available, ask about AWD fluid service history at the dealer.
Est. cost: $200–$450 AWD coupling fluid service Source: Nissan AWD coupling service specification; L34 field data

Body and Documentation

Open recall compliance — 23V628 and 25V437

High Attention
Two verified L34 recalls: rear camera wiring harness (23V628/R23C3, Platinum, 2019–2021) and VC-Turbo engine bearing (25V437, 2019–2020 VC-Turbo). An uncompleted 25V437 on a VC-Turbo car represents potential engine failure.
Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before signing. Both 23V628 and 25V437 must show as completed (where applicable). Unresolved recalls = negotiate a price reduction or have the dealer complete the repair first.
Est. cost: Free (recall repairs at Nissan dealers) Source: NHTSA recalls 23V628 (September 2023) and 25V437 (June 2025)

Water intrusion and sunroof drain check (if sunroof equipped)

Monitor
The L34, like the prior gen, can have clogged front sunroof drain tubes that route down the A-pillar. Blocked drains flood the footwell and can destroy the BCM and ADAS modules. Check the trunk seal as well for soft gasket leaks.
Press the front carpet in both footwells and the trunk floor — no moisture or musty odor. On sunroof-equipped cars, pour 200 mL of water at each front drain and confirm exit under the bumper within 5 seconds.
Est. cost: $80–$250 drain clearing and carpet drying Source: L34 Altima owner reports (NissanTalk, Reddit r/Nissan)

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

25V-437
VC-Turbo Engine Bearings — 2.0L Only, 443,899 Units
Engine bearings with manufacturing defects can cause engine failure, and a bearing failure can breach the block and release hot oil, adding a fire risk. The scope is the 2.0L VC-Turbo only — the 2.5L PR25DD does not share it, exactly as this checklist's VIN note says. The remedy is an ECM software reprogram rather than a mechanical repair, so confirm it from the VIN; owner letters were mailed 2026-04-08.
19V-316
Fuel Pump Lock Ring Not Engaged — 2.5L Front-Drive Only, FMVSS 301
A fuel pump lock ring that is not fully engaged can leak fuel near an ignition source. The scope is the front-wheel-drive 2.5L car — the mirror image of the bearing recall above, so the two engine families each carry one engine-specific campaign and they are different ones. Dealers tighten a disengaged ring or replace the pump if it has detached.
18V-922
Fuel Tube Retainer Clip Not Locked — Leak or Stall
The retainer clip joining the low-pressure fuel tube to the high-pressure fuel pump may not be locked in place. The tube can shift and leak, or disconnect entirely and stall the engine. A second and separate fuel-delivery campaign on the same model year as 19V-316, with a different mechanism — so "the fuel recall was done" is an incomplete answer on this car.
19V-235
Mislocated Brake Switch Bracket — Brake Lights Stay On and the Shifter Can Leave Park
A brake switch bracket in the wrong position can hold the switch on. Two consequences: the brake lights stay lit when nobody is braking, and the shift lever can be moved out of Park without the brake pedal pressed, which is a rollaway. Dealers inspect and fit a new brake pedal as needed. Easy to check on a walkaround — have someone watch the rear while you sit with your foot off the pedal.
23V-628
Damaged Rear Camera Harness — FMVSS 111
A damaged camera harness can distort or cut off the rearview image on 2019-2021 Altima and 2020-2021 Sentra. Dealers inspect the camera and harness and replace them if damaged, or apply protective tape and reroute the harness if not. The second of two separate camera campaigns on this car.
19V-654
Rear Camera Image Can Be Switched Off and Stay Off — FMVSS 111
On a broad set of 2018-2019 Nissan and Infiniti models the rear camera display setting can be changed so the image does not appear, and the setting persists across drive cycles. A blank reverse screen on a test drive is therefore a settings question before it is a hardware question — the previous owner may simply have turned it off. Dealers update the camera settings software.
21V-169
Over-Cured Continental Tires — 36,141 Units
Tires cured too long in production can develop a sidewall break or belt edge separation and lose air suddenly. 36,141 units across 2019-2020 Altima and 2018-2021 Titan. Practically, this is the one recall class a used buyer can usually discount on a car this age: the original tires are long gone. If the VIN still shows it open, that means nobody logged an inspection, not that the tires on the car are bad.

What the VIN tells you

The L34 Altima offers two distinct engine families: the 2.5L PR25DD (standard on S/SV/SL and all AWD trims) and the 2.0L VC-Turbo KR20DDET (SR and Platinum FWD only). AWD is exclusive to 2.5L models — a VC-Turbo Altima is always FWD. Use /decode/ to confirm the engine from the 8th VIN digit before inspecting; VC-Turbo units carry a separate oil-consumption and bearing-recall risk that the 2.5L does not share.
