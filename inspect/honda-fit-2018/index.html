Safety Recalls

Open recalls — VIN check (no Fit of this generation is a Takata car)

Monitor
Honda's Fit Takata scope is 2009-2014 — the GE-generation car. No GK Fit build date received a recalled inflator, so "some builds" is not a real distinction here. What the 2018 car does have is the Denso fuel pump campaign, which is the one to check.
Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before any test drive. Any open campaign = do not purchase until a dealer has closed it; the work is free and completion is logged against the VIN.
Est. cost: Free under recall Source: Honda Takata affected-model list (hondanews.com fact sheet), verified 2026-08-09

Documentation

CVT service history — the real walk-away document

Critical
CVT fluid history is the document that decides this purchase. Honda calls for fluid service around every 30,000 miles and a GK Fit with no receipts has almost certainly never had it done.
Request CVT fluid receipts, then run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. No CVT records = walk away.
Est. cost: $120-$180 fluid service / $3,500-$5,000 CVT replacement if neglected Source: Honda CVT service schedule

Pump impeller may deform and fail — stall risk. Covers 2018–2019 Fit; free replacement, verify by VIN.

What the VIN tells you

Engine-code correction — the US-market 2018 Fit (GK gen) uses the naturally-aspirated 1.5L L15B1 (130hp DOHC i-VTEC), not the turbocharged L15B7; the L15B7 is the Civic/CR-V 1.5T and only appears in a Fit as an aftermarket swap. With a single engine there is nothing to disambiguate by VIN — Honda carries the model/engine in the 6th VIN character and the 8th digit encodes grade/body within the line, not the powertrain. The variable worth confirming is the transmission: a 6-speed manual was offered on lower trims while EX/EX-L/Touring (and most builds) are CVT — verify the HCF-2 CVT-fluid service history.
