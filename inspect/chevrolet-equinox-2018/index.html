L / LS / LT / Premier (3rd gen, 2018-2024)
Watchful

The 3rd-gen Equinox was a significant step up from the 2nd gen — but the dominant engine, the 1.5L LFV turbo, has documented oil consumption and timing chain stretch issues that buyers must investigate. The 2.0L LSY is the stronger used pick. The 1.6L diesel is uncommon and requires DEF system maintenance. Avoid the 1.5T if oil change records are incomplete.

Engine / oil consumption (1.5T)

Powertrain

1.5L LYX turbo oil consumption

High Attention
The 1.5T LYX has documented oil consumption on higher-mileage examples (80k+). Piston ring wear causes oil burning; check engine light may follow with catalyst codes.
Pull the oil dipstick on 1.5T cars before driving. Low oil between changes is the key indicator. Ask for service records — changes more frequent than 5k miles suggest known consumption.
Est. cost: $0–$1,500 under extended warranty / $4,500–$7,000 engine replacement Source: NHTSA owner complaints (1.5T oil consumption); GM bulletin number unverified

1.5L LYX timing chain stretch

High Attention
Timing chain on the LYX can stretch prematurely with low oil or extended intervals. Rattle at cold start (first 3–5 seconds) is the audible warning.
Cold-start the engine (after overnight sit). A rattle in the first few seconds of startup that clears is early stretch; sustained rattle is advanced failure.
Est. cost: $1,800–$3,200 timing chain kit + guides Source: GM 1.5T Ecotec chain-wear pattern; LYX-specific bulletin unverified

Automatic transmission fluid condition - and no, there is no CVT

Monitor
No 2018 Equinox has a CVT. The 1.5T uses a six-speed automatic and the 2.0T a nine-speed, and both require factory-spec fluid. Brown, burnt, or opaque fluid means service is overdue. Treat any "Equinox CVT" guidance you find online as describing a later car, not this one.
Read the 8th VIN digit first so you know which gearbox you are testing, then check fluid condition at the fill plug and drive for flare or gear hunting from cold.
Est. cost: $200–$450 fluid service / $3,500–$5,500 transmission replacement Source: Consumer Guide + GM Authority 2018 Equinox specifications

Electrical

Infotainment software and MyLink freeze

Low Risk
MyLink infotainment on 2018 Equinox has known freeze and reboot loop issues. OTA updates and dealer reflash campaigns were issued.
Test Bluetooth, Apple CarPlay, backup camera, and all audio sources. Reboot during test = software issue.
Est. cost: $0 software update Source: GM campaigns

HVAC

A/C compressor failure

Monitor
A/C compressor failure rate is above average for this generation — typically at 70k–120k miles.
Run A/C at idle for 5+ minutes. Vent temp should reach low 40s F. Clicking from under hood is compressor clutch wear.
Est. cost: $800–$1,600 compressor replacement Source: NHTSA consumer complaints

Suspension

Front strut and sway bar link wear

Low Risk
Clunking over bumps from the front is typically worn sway bar end links — common at 60k+ miles.
Drive over a speed bump slowly. Clunk from front = end link or strut mount. Bounce test at front corners.
Est. cost: $150–$350 end links / $400–$700 struts Source: Common wear pattern

Documentation

Recall verification and oil consumption documentation

High Attention
Check the VIN for open campaigns, and separately ask about GM service campaign 18208 (oil consumption on the 1.5T). Oil consumption is the key disqualifier on this vehicle.
Run VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls. For 1.5T cars: require proof the oil consumption campaign was addressed.
Est. cost: Walk away from 1.5T cars with undocumented high-mileage oil issues Source: NHTSA + GM service records

What the VIN tells you

On a 2018 Equinox the 8th VIN digit is the engine, and it is the most important character on the car, because the three engines have almost nothing in common. Single-variable decodes at vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov, holding 2GNAXJE and the J tail constant and changing only position 8: 'V' = LYX, 1.5L gasoline turbo; 'X' = LTG, 2.0L gasoline turbo; '8', '9' and 'U' all return the 1.6L turbodiesel, which NHTSA carries under three submission codes (LWW twice, LH7 once) for what is one engine. Those five characters are the complete allowed set - feed position 8 anything outside it and vPIC answers with Possible values (8:89UVX), so there is no sixth engine hiding in this VIN. Two corrections to our own record came out of that. The 1.5T is GM's LYX, not the LFV: the LFV is the 160-hp 1.5T from the Malibu and Cruze, the Equinox got the 170-hp LYX, and GM Authority's 2018 Equinox specification table gives the same RPO independently. The 2.0T is the LTG, not the LSY - the LSY is a later GM engine and never shipped in a 2018 Equinox. Decode before you believe any transmission advice about this car, too: no 2018 Equinox has a CVT. The 1.5T uses a six-speed automatic and the 2.0T a nine-speed, and Consumer Guide and GM Authority list it that way, so Equinox CVT guidance is describing a different car. Run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov before purchase - a 1.6 diesel also brings DEF and emissions hardware history the gasoline cars do not have.
