2018 BMW 3 Series
320i / 328i / 330i / 340i (F30, 2012-2018)
High Attention

The F30 3 Series is one of the most satisfying driving experiences available used — but maintenance costs are above-average and must be budgeted honestly. The B48 4-cylinder is the most cost-effective engine. The N20 in 2012–2015 cars had timing chain issues; the B48 (2016+) resolved most of them. Budget $1,500–$3,000/year for maintenance on any used BMW. Pre-purchase inspection by a BMW specialist is non-negotiable.

Powertrain

N20 timing chain (2012–2015 only)

Critical
The N20 2.0T engine used in 2012–2015 328i has a documented upper timing chain guide failure. Rattling at startup on a warm engine is a pre-failure sign. 2016+ B48 does not share this issue.
2016+ cars with B48: lower risk. For any 2012–2015: cold-start the engine and listen for startup rattle. Ask for timing chain service records.
Est. cost: $1,500–$3,000 timing chain / guide replacement Source: BMW N20 class action

B48 / B58 coolant system (water pump and thermostat)

High Attention
BMW electric water pumps and expansion tanks fail at 80k–120k miles — often without warning. Loss of coolant quickly leads to overheating.
Check coolant level and inspect expansion tank for cracks. Any low coolant without external leak = suspect water pump or tank.
Est. cost: $600–$1,200 water pump + thermostat Source: BMW electric water pump known failure

Oil consumption and VANOS seals

Monitor
BMW engines consume oil by design — 1 qt per 1,000 miles is considered "normal" by BMW. VANOS solenoid seals leak at 80k+ miles.
Pull oil dipstick before driving. Ask seller how often oil is added between changes.
Est. cost: $0–$500 VANOS solenoid seals Source: BMW consumption standard

Transmission

ZF 8HP automatic fluid (DCT on M models)

Monitor
BMW specifies "lifetime" fluid — but independent shops recommend change at 60k. Low-speed shudder indicates fluid degradation.
Test low-speed maneuvering for shudder.
Est. cost: $400–$600 fluid service Source: BMW independent shop consensus

Electronics

iDrive system and control module faults

Monitor
F30 iDrive can accumulate module fault codes that are expensive to diagnose and repair.
Have the car scanned with BMW-compatible ISTA or BIMMERCODE before purchase. Any uncleared module faults are a negotiation point.
Est. cost: $200–$2,000+ depending on fault Source: Pre-purchase BMW scan standard

Chassis

Front control arms and bushings

High Attention
F30 control arm bushings and ball joints wear at 60k–80k miles — earlier than competitors.
Lift the car and inspect front suspension components, or have shop do it.
Est. cost: $800–$2,000 front suspension refresh Source: F30 common repair list

Documentation

BMW specialist pre-purchase inspection required

Critical
Do not buy any used BMW without a pre-purchase inspection by a BMW-trained independent shop with ISTA access.
Budget $150–$200 for PPI. Non-negotiable on any F30.
Est. cost: $150–$200 PPI / $2,000–$15,000+ deferred repairs discovered Source: BMW ownership standard

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

18V-465
Crankshaft Sensor Firmware — Stall
Incorrect sensor firmware can stall the engine while driving. Covers certain 2018-2019 330i/330i xDrive/340i/340i xDrive (small population, ~5,309 across BMW/MINI). Dealer replaces the sensor.
21V-586
Diesel High-Pressure Fuel Pump (328d only)
HPFP failure can stall the engine — applies only to 2014-2018 328d/328d xDrive diesels. Dealer replaces the pump.
21V-907
Diesel EGR Cooler — Fire Risk (328d only)
EGR cooler can leak internally; coolant plus soot can smolder and melt the intake manifold. 2013-2018 328d variants; supersedes 18V-755 and was expanded again by 25V-700 (2025) — insist on the LATEST campaign closed by VIN.
Multiple
Open recalls — VIN check
Campaigns vary by build date. Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and require every open recall closed before purchase.
