Watchful

The 10th-gen Accord (2018–2022) is one of Honda's best engineering achievements — sharp to drive, spacious, and well-built. The 1.5T L15B7 has the same cold-weather oil dilution history as the Civic. The 2.0T K20C1 is the Sport 2.0T and Touring trim choice — generally free of oil dilution but requires verified CVT or 10AT service. Outstanding midsize sedan value.

What the VIN tells you

Two turbocharged engines, two different inspection priorities — and on the 10th-gen Accord the engine is set by trim. The 1.5L L15B7 turbo (192 hp) powers LX, EX, EX-L, and the base Sport; it shares the 10th-gen Civic's cold-climate fuel-in-oil dilution history, so on ANY 1.5T pull the dipstick (oil at or above MAX = active dilution) and confirm Honda's PCM/software update (TSB 19-049 class) is applied ($5,500+ engine if severe). The 2.0L K20C1 turbo (252 hp) is fitted only to the Sport 2.0T and Touring 2.0T; it does not share the dilution issue but pairs with the 10-speed automatic — verify its fluid-service history. Identify the engine three ways before you negotiate: read the trim badge, note the 2.0T's deeper exhaust tone, and — authoritatively — decode the full VIN at autovetting.com/decode, which returns the engine family from NHTSA's vPIC database. The 10th VIN digit gives the model year: J=2018, K=2019, L=2020, M=2021, N=2022.
