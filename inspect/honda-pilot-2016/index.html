2016 Honda Pilot
LX / EX / EX-L / Touring / Elite (3rd gen, 2016–2022)
Medium Attention

The 3rd-gen Pilot (2016–2022) is a capable three-row SUV that arrived with two meaningful first-generation problems. The ZF 9-speed automatic (2016–2017) had calibration issues causing harsh, hunting shifts — software updates improved but did not fully solve this. The 3.5L V6 VCM system also destroys the right-rear motor mount prematurely, the same issue seen on Accord V6s. 2018 and newer are better-sorted with revised 9-speed tuning from the factory. Buy a well-maintained 2018+ if the budget allows; if buying 2016–2017, confirm the 9-speed software updates have been applied and check the motor mounts carefully. On recalls, 2016 is the model year to scrutinise: NHTSA lists eight campaigns, including two separate fuel-tank replacements, the 23V-751 connecting-rod-bearing engine recall that skips the 2017 car entirely, and the April 2026 rear-subframe corrosion campaign for salt-belt vehicles.

Power Train / Transmission

Transmission

9-speed automatic — hunting and harsh shifts (2016–2017)

High Attention
The first two years of the 3rd-gen Pilot used a ZF 9-speed automatic that was poorly calibrated at launch. Owners reported harsh downshifts, gear hunting on gentle grades, jerky stop-and-go behavior, and delayed throttle response. Honda issued multiple software updates. 2018+ Pilots received revised factory calibration and are meaningfully smoother.
Test drive at highway speeds (55–70 mph) on a gently rolling road — watch for unprompted downshifts on slight grades. Also test in light city traffic: gradual acceleration from 5 to 30 mph should feel smooth. If buying a 2016–2017, ask a Honda dealer to confirm all 9-speed software updates are applied via VIN check.
Est. cost: $0 if software update resolves / $3,000–$5,000 valve body or transmission rebuild if mechanical damage Source: Honda TSB (multiple ZF 9HP calibration campaigns) + NHTSA complaints

6-speed automatic (LX trim) — reliable baseline

Low Risk
LX trim Pilots used a conventional Honda 6-speed automatic that is far more predictable than the 9-speed. If the 9-speed's reputation is a concern, LX is the lowest-risk transmission choice.
Standard test: drive through all gears smoothly. No hesitation, no unexpected downshifts, clean engagement into Reverse. Verify ATF-DW1 Honda fluid has been serviced every 30,000 miles.
Est. cost: $150 fluid service / $2,500–$4,000 rebuild if neglected Source: Honda Pilot 3rd gen transmission documentation

VCM & Engine Mounts

VCM right-rear motor mount wear

High Attention
The 3.5L V6 uses Honda's Variable Cylinder Management (VCM), switching between 3, 4, and 6 cylinders based on load. The cylinder transition vibration prematurely destroys the right-rear motor mount — typically by 80,000–100,000 miles. This is the same issue as on the Accord V6, but on a heavier vehicle with more total engine mass.
With the engine running in Drive, foot on brake, hood open: watch the engine for visible movement at idle. Minor vibration is normal; significant rocking or a visible gap in the right-rear mount = replacement needed. A mechanic on a lift can compress the mount by hand — it should resist firmly.
Est. cost: $400–$700 right-rear mount / $1,200+ if all mounts are affected Source: Honda VCM motor mount pattern (shared with Accord V6)

VCM deactivation device (positive modification)

Monitor
Many Pilot owners install aftermarket "VCM Muzzler" or similar devices to prevent the V6 from dropping to 3-cylinder mode, protecting the motor mounts. This is widely regarded as a smart preventive modification.
Ask if a VCM Muzzler or similar has been installed. If yes — this is a positive sign of a proactive owner. If not, factor future motor mount wear into your cost estimate.
Est. cost: $50–$100 device — preventive investment Source: Honda VCM Muzzler documentation

Safety Systems

Honda Sensing false-positive braking (2016–2018)

Monitor
Honda Sensing (collision mitigation, lane keeping, adaptive cruise) became standard on most 3rd-gen Pilot trims. Early units had false-positive CMBS events — unexpected braking on highway with no actual hazard. Honda issued radar and camera software updates.
During a highway test drive, verify Honda Sensing does not activate unexpectedly on a clear road. Ask if Honda Sensing software updates have been applied (dealer VIN check is free).
Est. cost: $0 software update / $1,500–$3,000 camera or radar module if replacement needed Source: Honda TSB (Honda Sensing calibration, multiple years)

Open recall check — airbag and restraint campaigns

Monitor
The 3rd-gen Pilot has had recall campaigns related to airbag sensing and seat belt pretensioners. Not all vehicles have been repaired.
Enter the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls before seeing the vehicle. Any open airbag or restraint recall = require dealer repair before purchase.
Est. cost: Free dealer repair Source: NHTSA recall database

Interior & Infotainment

CarPlay / Android Auto — 2019+ only

Low Risk
The 3rd-gen Pilot was sold without CarPlay / Android Auto from 2016–2018. Honda released a dealer retrofit kit for some 2018 models, but earlier cars require aftermarket head units if wireless projection is a priority. Not a defect — just a generational feature gap.
If CarPlay matters to you, confirm model year and trim support before the test drive. 2019+ EX and above have it standard. 2016–2018 require the Honda retrofit or aftermarket.
Est. cost: $0 awareness item / $200–$800 Honda retrofit or aftermarket unit Source: Honda Pilot trim/feature history

Second-row captain's seat walk-in mechanism (Touring / Elite)

Monitor
Power second-row captain's chairs (Elite and some Touring trims) have a walk-in mechanism that can bind, resist, or fail to return to position. Motors and track mechanisms are complex and expensive to repair.
Fully cycle both second-row captain's chairs through their walk-in positions. They should move smoothly forward, lock in the walk-in position, and return fully to the seated position with a firm click. Grinding, resistance, or incomplete return = mechanism service needed.
Est. cost: $300–$600 track/mechanism repair per side Source: Honda Pilot 3rd gen owner reports (Touring/Elite)

Infotainment screen responsiveness (2016–2018)

Monitor
The 2016–2018 Pilot infotainment abandons physical controls for a touch-heavy interface — the screen hardware is generally reliable, but early units did see responsiveness degradation.
Tap through every screen menu and test voice command activation. Response should be under 1 second per touch. Phantom taps or frozen screens = head unit issue.
Est. cost: $400–$1,200 head unit replacement if screen fails Source: Honda Pilot 3rd gen infotainment reports

Exterior & Mechanical

Power tailgate motor and latch (EX and above)

Low Risk
The power tailgate on EX and above can slow down or stall from a weak motor or misadjusted control arms — especially on cold-weather cars. The latch mechanism can also fail to fully engage.
Open and close the power tailgate at least three times. It should travel smoothly in both directions and latch with a single firm click. Hesitation mid-travel or failure to fully close = motor or latch service.
Est. cost: $150–$400 motor or latch adjustment / $600–$900 full motor replacement Source: Honda Pilot owner reports

Undercarriage corrosion — brake lines and heat shields (salt-belt)

Monitor
The Pilot uses a unibody construction — resistant to the severe frame rust that affects truck-based SUVs. However, salt-belt cars can develop corrosion on brake lines, exhaust hangers, and heat shields.
On a lift, inspect the undercarriage for flaking or pitted corrosion on brake lines and fuel lines. Surface rust on heat shields is cosmetic; any brake line that has pitted into the metal = immediate replacement before driving.
Est. cost: $0 cosmetic / $300–$1,500 if brake lines need replacement Source: Honda Pilot salt-belt inspection pattern

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

15V-424
Third-Row Seat Belt Can Be Trapped
Certain 2016 Pilots built mid-2015. A third-row seat belt can become trapped between the seat and the sideliner, so an occupant may not be properly restrained. Dealer inspects and repairs. Free repair.
15V-668
Instrument Cluster Software — Delayed Warning Lights
Certain 2016 Pilots. When the tire-pressure monitor, ABS or stability control malfunctions, the warning light may not appear until the ignition is cycled — a failure of three federal safety standards. Dealer updates the cluster software. Free repair.
16V-417
Fuel Tank Weld Separation — Fire Risk
Certain 2015-2016 Acura MDX, 2015-2016 Odyssey and 2016 Pilot. Weak welds can let the fuel tank separate and leak. Dealer replaces the tank. Free repair.
17V-219
Leaking Fuel Tank (2016 Pilot 2WD and AWD)
A second, Pilot-specific tank campaign covering 2016 2WD and AWD vehicles: the fuel tank can leak, and a leak near an ignition source raises fire risk. Dealer replaces the tank. Two separate tank recalls apply to this model year — a closed 16V-417 does not clear 17V-219. Free repair.
21V-932
Hood Latch Striker Can Separate
Certain 2016-2019 Pilot, 2019 Passport and 2017-2020 Ridgeline. The hood latch striker can be damaged and separate, letting the hood open while driving and block the driver’s view. Dealer repairs the striker or replaces the hood. Free repair.
23V-751
Connecting Rod Bearing Wear — Engine Seizure
The one to check first. A manufacturing error can make the connecting rod bearing wear and seize, damaging the engine and risking stall or fire. 248,999 units. Scope is specific: 2016, 2018 and 2019 Pilot — the 2017 Pilot is not in this campaign. Dealer inspects and repairs or replaces the engine. Free repair.
23V-858
Denso In-Tank Fuel Pump Failure
Wide Honda and Acura campaign including 2015-2020 Pilot, and the last in a series of Denso fuel-pump campaigns — earlier waves covered other models, so this is the one that reaches the Pilot. The in-tank fuel pump impeller can fail and stall the engine while driving. Dealer replaces the fuel pump module. Free repair.
26V-365
Rear Subframe Corrosion — Salt-Belt States Only
Opened April 2026 and the newest campaign on this car: 880,514 units across 2016-2022 Pilot, 2017-2023 Ridgeline, 2019-2023 Passport and 2014-2020 Acura MDX. Rear subframes can corrode at the suspension mounting points and a control arm can fail, causing loss of control. Limited to vehicles originally sold in 22 states plus DC (the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic and Midwest salt belt) — a Phoenix car is unlikely to be covered, but a car that moved here from Ohio may be. Dealer inspects, reinforces and repairs. Free repair.
