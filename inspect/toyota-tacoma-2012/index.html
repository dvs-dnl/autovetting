Structure / Frame

Frame & Structure (mandatory first stop)

Frame rust (the inspection that decides everything else)

High Attention
Pre-2016 Tacomas in salt-belt states have well-documented frame corrosion. Toyota ran a buyback program through 2018 that absorbed the worst trucks. Any frame rust beyond surface scale = walk away.
Crawl under and inspect the entire frame, especially around the rear leaf spring shackles and rear cross-member. Tap with a screwdriver — solid thunk = OK, soft or flaking = bad. AZ/TX/SoCal trucks are almost always clean; verify via Carfax if the truck has lived east of the Rockies.
Est. cost: $0 to walk away / $5,000+ for new frame (rare these days) Source: Toyota frame inspection campaign + NHTSA

Rear leaf springs (sag and rust)

Monitor
Tacoma leafs sag and rust over time, especially on trucks that towed or hauled. Sagging rear = poor ride and bottoming out.
Measure the gap between the bump stop and axle — should be 2.5-3" empty. Inspect leaf packs for broken leaves or rust pitting. Listen for clunks while reversing — broken leaf pin is common.
Est. cost: $200-$400 add-a-leaf / $600-$1,200 new pack Source: Standard wear pattern

Powertrain — 4.0L V6 (1GR-FE)

Lower ball joints (wear item, not a recall on this truck)

High Attention
Toyota's lower-ball-joint recall (NHTSA 05V-225, campaign 50J) covered 2001-2004 Tacoma, 2002-2004 Tundra and Sequoia and 2001-2002 4Runner — first-gen trucks only. A 2012 Tacoma is nowhere near that campaign, so treat the ball joints purely as a wear item: they carry the front suspension load and a failed one can cause loss of steering.
Jack the front wheel and rock top-bottom (12-6). Any movement = worn. Also grab the wheel at 9-3 to separate ball-joint play from tie-rod play, and check the boots for splits.
Est. cost: $300-$500 per side parts + labor Source: Wear pattern; NHTSA 05V-225 cited for scope only

Carbon buildup (port-injected, less common)

Low Risk
The 1GR-FE is port-injected (not direct-injected) — meaning carbon buildup is minor compared to modern DI engines.
Verify smooth idle and clean throttle response. Compression test if you want to confirm — should be even across all 6 cylinders.
Est. cost: $200 throttle body cleaning if needed Source: Engine design

Timing chain (no scheduled replacement)

Low Risk
The 1GR-FE uses a timing chain — no scheduled belt service required. Generally bulletproof to 300,000+ miles.
Listen for chain rattle at cold start — should be brief or absent. Inspect chain tensioner area for oil weeping.
Est. cost: $1,500+ if tensioner replacement needed (rare) Source: Toyota 1GR-FE engine docs

Automatic transmission (A750F 5-speed)

Low Risk
The 5-speed automatic is rock-solid. Toyota called the fluid "lifetime" — owner consensus is to change it at 60k and 120k anyway.
Confirm fluid history. Test all shift points in a 20-min drive. Listen for any clunk into reverse or harsh 1-2 shift.
Est. cost: $250-$350 fluid service Source: Toyota service docs + community consensus

Suspension & Steering

Front shocks and bilstein conversion popular

Low Risk
OEM shocks are worn by 100k. Many owners install Bilstein 5100s or 6112s as a popular upgrade.
Push down on each front corner — should rebound once and settle. Bouncing = blown shocks. Aftermarket shocks are usually an upgrade, not a downgrade — verify the brand.
Est. cost: $400-$800 Bilstein conversion (DIY) Source: Tacoma community consensus

Steering rack — minor leaks possible

Low Risk
Power steering rack can develop slow seal leaks at high mileage. Hydraulic steering = no electric assist failures.
Check PS fluid level. Inspect rack boots for tears or fluid weeping. Listen for whine when turning.
Est. cost: $1,000-$1,500 rack rebuild (uncommon below 200k) Source: Standard wear pattern

Body & Electrical

Bed corrosion (composite bed = no rust)

Low Risk
Tacoma uses a composite bed — no rust issues. Inspect bed mounting points to the frame, however.
Inspect bed-to-frame mounting brackets for rust or cracking. Composite itself can crack at high stress points (tie-down anchors).
Est. cost: $200-$500 bracket replacement Source: Truck-specific design

Door rust (lower edges)

Monitor
Lower door edges can rust through, especially on salt-belt trucks. Common Tacoma issue.
Run a finger along the bottom edge of each door — feel for rust scale or bubbling paint. Inside the door, the lower drain holes may be plugged.
Est. cost: $200-$400 per door panel repair / $600+ replacement Source: NHTSA reports

Backup camera reliability (Limited and TRD trims)

Low Risk
Higher trims have a backup camera that can fog, flicker, or fail. Easy aftermarket replacement.
Test the backup camera — should produce a clear image immediately. Foggy = moisture intrusion, may need replacement.
Est. cost: $60-$200 aftermarket camera (DIY) Source: Standard wear

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

14V-054
Brake Actuator / VSC-ABS Software
2012-2013 Tacoma (also 2012-2013 Lexus RX 350 and 2012 RAV4): resistance inside the brake actuator can illuminate the VSC, ABS and traction-control lights and disable those systems. Skid-control ECU software update. This is the only factory Toyota campaign on a 2012 Tacoma — dash lights on during the test drive are the tell.
17V-425
Accessory Hood Scoop (Southeast Toyota trucks)
2011-2016 Tacoma and 4Runner with a dealer-installed SET hood scoop: the adhesive can weaken and the scoop can depart the truck at speed. Remedy mechanically fastens it. SET17B — only trucks distributed through Southeast Toyota (FL/GA/AL/NC/SC/TN).
14V-475
Tire Placard — Accessory Wheels
2008-2014 Tacoma and FJ Cruiser fitted with accessory wheels/tires before first sale: the placard lists the wrong spare size and/or cold inflation pressure (FMVSS 110). Corrective overlay sticker. Check the door-jamb placard against the wheels actually on the truck.
13V-494
Spare-Wheel Lug Nuts (18-inch Maverick alloys)
2012-2013 Tacoma with SET 18-inch Maverick alloy wheels: the spare needs a different lug-nut style than the road wheels, and those nuts were never supplied. Wrong nuts can damage the wheel and let it come loose. Remedy = warning label plus a bag of correct nuts — look for the bag.
13V-123
Load-Capacity Label (Southeast Toyota)
2010-2013 Tacoma among a long Southeast Toyota list: the weight-added label is outside the 1% accuracy allowed by FMVSS 110, so the payload figure on the door is wrong. Corrected label.
13V-014
Occupant-Sensing Calibration (SET-modified trucks)
2009-2012 Tacoma modified by Southeast Toyota with leather covers, seat heaters or headrest DVD: the front passenger occupant-sensing system may never have been recalibrated, so the passenger airbag can fail to deploy or deploy wrongly. Dealers retest and recalibrate. Aftermarket-looking leather on a Tacoma of this era is the flag.
12V-158
Tire Placard (Gulf States Toyota)
2012 Tacoma distributed by Gulf States Toyota (TX/OK/AR/LA/MS): inaccurate spare tire size and cold inflation pressure on the placard (FMVSS 110). Corrected placard mailed to owners.
Multiple
Open recalls — VIN check
Five of the seven campaigns above are regional-distributor or accessory campaigns, so whether a given truck is covered depends on where it was first sold and what was bolted to it. Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and require every open recall closed before purchase.
