2020 Jeep Gladiator
Sport / Overland / Rubicon / Mojave (JT, 2020-present)
Watchful

The JT Gladiator is the only mid-size convertible pickup truck on the market — it combines Wrangler JL capability with a 5-foot bed. The 3.6L Pentastar V6 is proven. Main buyer concerns: Death Wobble risk from worn front end components (same as Wrangler), throttle position sensor failure on early builds, and eTorque BSG concerns on equipped models. Strong buy for buyers who need both off-road capability and a bed.

Steering & Suspension

Death Wobble — front end wear

Critical
JT Gladiator inherits the JL Wrangler platform's Death Wobble risk from worn track bar, drag link, or worn front end components.
Test at 55–75 mph — any shimmy or oscillation after a bump = Death Wobble. Inspect track bar mounting bolts and bushings.
Est. cost: $300–$1,500 front end repair depending on components Source: Wrangler/Gladiator Death Wobble pattern

What the VIN tells you

On the JT Gladiator the 8th VIN digit is the engine character, and what it cannot tell you matters more than what it can. Measured by holding a constructed 2020 Gladiator VIN constant and varying one position at a time through NHTSA vPIC: ‘G’ returns the 3.6L Pentastar V6, gasoline, six cylinders — and on a 2020 truck that is the only engine Jeep built. Two controls were run to show position 8 is doing the work: changing position 9 left the engine at 3.6L (it is the check digit, not powertrain data), and changing position 7 moved the reported trim from Sport to Rubicon while the engine held at 3.6L, so trim and engine are encoded separately. Now the caution. vPIC will also answer for characters Jeep never built into a 2020 Gladiator: off the same 2020 VIN, ‘M’ returns a 3.0L diesel V6 and ‘J’ returns a 6.4L gasoline V8. The 3.0L EcoDiesel did not reach the Gladiator until the 2021 model year, and no Gladiator was sold with the 6.4L at all. The decoder is reading Chrysler’s shared engine table rather than validating the combination, so a decode that returns an engine is not proof that engine was offered — and if a 2020 Gladiator is advertised as a diesel, the listing has the model year wrong, which is a price question and not a trim question. Everything else on this page applies either way: the Pentastar is the proven engine, and the front-end wear behind Death Wobble is where the inspection money goes. Run the full VIN at nhtsa.gov to confirm every open campaign is closed.
