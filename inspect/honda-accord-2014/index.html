Electrical System

Powertrain

CVT transmission shudder (4-cyl models)

High Attention
The 2014 4-cylinder Accord uses a CVT that can develop a low-speed shudder, especially above 90,000 miles or if the fluid has never been changed.
Drive at 25-40 mph in light throttle, then ease off and re-apply gently. Listen and feel for a rough vibration. Confirm the CVT fluid has been changed (Honda recommends every 30,000 miles; many owners skip this). Genuine Honda HCF-2 fluid only — universal CVT fluid causes this shudder.
Est. cost: $300 fluid service / $3,500-$5,000 if replacement needed Source: Owner reports + Honda TSB 14-027

CVT Hill Start Assist software glitch (TSB 16-049)

Monitor
Some 2014 CVT Accords have a Hill Start Assist software glitch that can briefly release the brake on hills. Honda issued a software update under TSB 16-049 — not a recall, so many cars never got it.
Ask if TSB 16-049 software update has been applied. If unknown, a Honda dealer can check via VIN in seconds. Free if the dealer applies the update during another service visit.
Est. cost: Free under TSB / $120 dealer diagnostic if you have to ask cold Source: Honda TSB 16-049

Carbon buildup on direct-injection 2.4L

Monitor
The K24W direct-injection 4-cylinder can develop intake valve carbon buildup at 80,000+ miles, causing rough idle and slight power loss. Generic mechanics often misdiagnose this as ignition.
Watch idle quality at cold startup and during long idles in traffic. A borescope inspection of intake valves is the definitive check. Ask whether any "induction service" or walnut-blast cleaning has been done.
Est. cost: $400-$700 walnut blast service if needed Source: Common to all DI engines; widely documented

Oil dilution from short-trip use (DI engines)

Monitor
Direct-injection 2.4L engines can develop oil dilution (raw fuel in the oil) if driven primarily on short trips under 15 minutes. Less common in warm climates, more in cold-belt cars.
Pull the dipstick and smell the oil. Strong gasoline smell = dilution. Ask the seller about driving patterns: if it's been a short-trip commuter and oil changes are stretched, walk away or budget for a near-term overhaul.
Est. cost: $0 if caught early (oil change) / bearing wear if chronic Source: Honda DI engine pattern

V6 timing chain (if equipped)

Low Risk
The 3.5L J35Y V6 uses a timing chain — no scheduled belt service required. Generally bulletproof.
Listen for any rattling at cold start. If chain stretches (rare on this engine), you may hear a brief rattle for 1-2 seconds before oil pressure builds.
Est. cost: $1,500-$2,500 if chain replacement needed (uncommon) Source: Honda J35Y engine documentation

V6 VCM motor mount wear (the V6's Achilles heel)

High Attention
The V6's Variable Cylinder Management (VCM) system runs on 3, 4, or 6 cylinders depending on load. The cylinder transitions create vibration that prematurely destroys the right rear motor mount — often by 80,000 miles — causing noticeable shake at idle. Enthusiasts install a "VCM Muzzler" device to disable VCM and extend mount life.
Idle the car in Drive with the brake pressed, hood up. Watch the engine — minor shake is normal; significant rocking or visible movement at the right rear mount means it's failing. A shop on a lift can confirm by checking mount integrity. Ask if a VCM Muzzler or similar has been installed.
Est. cost: $400-$700 right rear motor mount / $1,200+ all mounts Source: Honda V6 VCM widely documented (drive accord forum, NHTSA)

Electrical

Battery drain / starter motor (top NHTSA complaint)

High Attention
Electrical issues are the #1 owner complaint for this generation Accord — 376 NHTSA reports, primarily battery drain, weak starters, and intermittent no-start.
Test the battery age (sticker on battery) and confirm replacement if older than 4 years. Have the seller cold-start the car after it has sat overnight. Listen for slow crank. If keyless entry feels unresponsive, parasitic drain may be present.
Est. cost: $200-$400 battery / $400-$700 starter Source: NHTSA: 376 electrical complaints (top issue category)

VSA / ABS modulator failure (2013-15 chronic issue)

High Attention
A pattern across 2013-15 Accords: the ABS modulator (the brain that controls anti-lock + stability) fails internally, often triggered or accelerated by neglected brake fluid changes. This is the single most expensive surprise on this car if missed.
Look for VSA, ABS, or Brake warning lights on the dashboard. Ask whether brake fluid has been changed (Honda interval is 3 years). Hard-brake from 30 mph on loose gravel — pedal should pulse if ABS engages. No pulse can mean the system has been disabled by stored faults.
Est. cost: $1,800-$2,400 modulator replacement Source: NHTSA reports + Honda service bulletins

Bluetooth / HandsFreeLink (HFL) module

Monitor
The HFL module that powers Bluetooth can fail independently of the rest of the infotainment, killing all phone connectivity while the rest of the system works.
Pair a phone via Bluetooth and test BOTH call audio (incoming and outgoing) AND music streaming. Try a second phone if possible. Intermittent connection = HFL going.
Est. cost: $400-$800 module replacement Source: Honda forum-documented (driveaccord.net)

LaneWatch camera (EX, EX-L, Touring)

Low Risk
The passenger-side LaneWatch camera (mounted to the right mirror) can lose signal or display interference. Cheap to test, expensive to replace.
Activate the right turn signal. Image should appear immediately on the infotainment screen, clear and stable. No image, static, or flicker = camera or wiring fault.
Est. cost: $300-$500 camera / $800+ if wiring damaged Source: Honda forum-documented

Display screen / infotainment

Monitor
The dual-screen infotainment can develop ghost touches, freezes, or full screen failure — most common on EX-L and Touring with the upper navigation display.
Test every touch input on the lower screen. Toggle through every menu on the upper screen. Watch for sluggish response or random taps registering on their own. Ask if the unit has ever been replaced under warranty.
Est. cost: $800-$1,500 replacement (out of warranty) Source: NHTSA complaints + Honda TSB 15-091

HVAC & Climate

A/C condenser (front-mounted, road debris vulnerable)

Monitor
Honda mounts the A/C condenser at the very front of the vehicle with minimal protection. A single stone strike can pierce a fin and cause refrigerant loss. A surprisingly common warranty claim on this generation.
A/C should blow cold within 30 seconds at idle on a warm day. Inspect the condenser through the grille for visible damage — bent fins, oily residue, or refrigerant dye stains. If blowing warm, the leak is often the condenser, not the compressor.
Est. cost: $700-$1,200 condenser replacement Source: Honda warranty claim pattern

A/C compressor clutch noise

Monitor
Honda A/C compressors are known for a slight clutch engagement "tick" at cold start with A/C on. Light noise is normal Honda quirk; loud noise or constant on/off cycling is a failing clutch or low refrigerant.
Cold start engine with A/C set to MAX. Listen at engine bay for engagement tick. Constant cycling on/off within 30 seconds of running = low refrigerant (cheap fix) or failing clutch (expensive).
Est. cost: $600-$1,000 compressor / $80 refrigerant recharge if just low Source: Honda compressor pattern

HVAC blend door actuator

Low Risk
The actuator controlling driver-side temperature mix can develop a repetitive clicking sound or stop responding, leaving cabin stuck hot or cold.
Test temperature dial across full range and verify air actually changes. Listen for ticking from behind dashboard center. Driver and passenger zones independently.
Est. cost: $250-$450 actuator + labor Source: Common across Honda/Acura

Steering & Brakes

Electric Power Steering (EPS) intermittent assist

Monitor
Some 2014 Accords report intermittent EPS warning lights and brief loss of power-steering assist (138 NHTSA complaints).
Check for any steering warning lights on the dash. During the test drive, steering effort should be even and consistent. Notchy or vague feel can indicate an EPS column with hours left. Ask if EPS has ever been replaced.
Est. cost: $1,200-$1,800 EPS column replacement Source: NHTSA: 138 steering complaints

Brake judder from undersized front rotors (2013-15)

Monitor
2013-15 Accords shipped with marginal front rotors that warp prematurely — often by 40,000 miles — causing steering wheel shimmy when braking from highway speed. Honda quietly upsized the rotors in later years.
Brake firmly from 60 mph on a clear stretch of road. Steering wheel vibration = warped rotors. Replace with upgraded thicker aftermarket rotors to break the cycle.
Est. cost: $300-$500 rotors + pads (aftermarket recommended) Source: Drive Accord forum + Honda quiet redesign in 2016

Battery sensor recall (17V-418) + open-campaign check

High Attention
The real safety campaign on this car is 17V-418: the battery-sensor case on 2013-2016 Accords can admit water and short, which is a fire risk. Salt-belt cars also carry the 20V-769 drive-shaft recall, and 23V-858 covers the Denso fuel pump.
Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and require every open campaign closed before purchase. Ask for the dealer repair order showing 17V-418 (Honda KG0) was completed, not just the interim adhesive fix.
Est. cost: Free under recall Source: NHTSA recall campaigns 17V-418 / 20V-769 / 23V-858

Front brake pads & rotors (standard wear)

Low Risk
Standard wear. Most 2014 Accords need front pads and possibly rotors by 70,000-90,000 miles.
Ask for service records showing brake history. Look at pad thickness through the wheel spokes if possible. Listen for grinding or squealing.
Est. cost: $250-$450 pads + rotors front Source: Standard wear pattern

Body & Interior

Paint quality (thin clearcoat)

Low Risk
Honda's clearcoat is thinner than European brands. Minor chips and stone bruises common; major peeling is not.
Inspect hood, roof, and bumper edges in good light. Look for clearcoat haze or peeling. Minor wear is normal at this mileage.
Est. cost: $500-$2,000 for major repaint (cosmetic) Source: Common to all Hondas of this era

Sunroof drain tube clogging

Monitor
Sunroof drain channels at all four corners can clog with debris, backing water up into the headliner or down the A-pillar into the floor pan. Mold + electrical damage if undetected.
Open the sunroof and pour ~1 cup of water into the front-of-sunroof drain channel. Water should exit at the fender behind the front wheels within 10 seconds. Pooling = clogged. Check carpet for damp spots.
Est. cost: $100-$300 drain cleaning / $$$ if interior water damage exists Source: Common across all sunroof-equipped vehicles

Sport trim 18" rim damage

Low Risk
Sport trim 18" wheels are softer alloy than standard 17" and bend or crack from pothole impacts more easily. Check carefully on Sport-trim cars.
Run a hand around the inside lip of each wheel feeling for bends or cracks. Steering wheel pull at 65+ mph can indicate a bent rim. A shop balance will reveal weight imbalance from cracks.
Est. cost: $250-$500 per wheel repair or replacement Source: Honda Sport trim pattern

Driver seat bolster wear

Low Risk
The leather (EX-L, Touring) on the outer bolster of the driver seat is the most common interior wear point.
Look at the outer left edge of the driver seat for cracking, wear, or color loss. Purely cosmetic but indicates how the car was used.
Est. cost: $150-$400 for re-bolstering Source: Standard wear pattern

Suspension & Underbody

Front compliance bushings (lower control arm)

Monitor
At 80,000+ miles, the front compliance bushings can develop play, causing a clunk over bumps and uneven tire wear. Often dismissed by general mechanics as "just struts."
Drive over rough pavement and listen for clunks from the front. Check front tire inner edges for uneven wear. Ask a shop to inspect bushings visually on a lift — the rubber should be intact, not torn or oozing.
Est. cost: $400-$700 per side (parts + labor) Source: Common Honda wear item

Rear trailing arm bushing wear (high mileage)

Low Risk
At 100,000+ miles, rear trailing arm bushings can soften, causing slight rear-end wallow in cornering. Less common than front but worth checking on high-mileage cars.
Drive at 35-45 mph through a sharp corner. Rear should track cleanly. Floaty or vague rear feel suggests bushing wear. A shop on a lift can confirm visually.
Est. cost: $400-$700 per side Source: High-mileage Accord pattern

What the VIN tells you

8th VIN digit engine code: ‘F’ = 2.4L K24W I4 (4-cylinder, CVT — most common). ‘Y’ = 3.5L J35Y V6 (6-cylinder, 6AT — no CVT). These two engines have completely different inspection priorities: 4-cyl buyers must verify CVT fluid history (Honda HCF-2, 30k intervals) and confirm TSB 16-049 software update; V6 buyers must check VCM right-rear motor mount wear at idle and have no CVT concern. Always decode the full VIN at autovetting.com/decode to confirm engine family before applying this checklist.
