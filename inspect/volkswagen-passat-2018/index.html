S / SE / SEL / R-Line / V6 SE (B8 NMS)
Watchful

The NMS B8 Passat is built in Tennessee for the North American market — simpler and larger than the European B8, and positioned as a value alternative to Camry/Accord. The 1.8T and 2.0T EA888 engines share the timing chain tensioner concern. Note the transmission split: the four-cylinder cars use an Aisin TF-62SN six-speed torque-converter automatic, not a DSG — only the 3.6 VR6 got a dual-clutch. And no 2018 Passat is in any Takata campaign; VW Takata scope for the US Passat is MY2012-2014. Strong value in the used midsize sedan segment for buyers who are budget-conscious with their own PPI.

Transmission — DSG only on the VR6

Monitor
The 1.8T and 2.0T Passat uses an Aisin TF-62SN six-speed torque-converter automatic. Only the 3.6 VR6 got the six-speed DSG. Budget the service the car in front of you actually needs — DSG shudder advice does not apply to a four-cylinder Passat.
Confirm the engine first. VR6: test stop-and-go under 15 mph for DSG shudder. Four-cylinder: test for flare or hunting from cold and check the fluid service record.
Est. cost: $150–$250 Aisin service / $350–$600 DSG service (VR6 only) Source: VW model-year specifications

Open recalls — VIN check (the Takata claim does not apply here)

Monitor
The Passat is the one nameplate where the usual reasoning fails, so be precise. Volkswagen is the only manufacturer NHTSA pushed into recalling desiccated Takata inflators, which is why late VWs appear in a Takata campaign at all — but that recall, 24V-834, lists the 2017-2019 Beetle, the 2006-2007 B6 Passat and the 2012-2014 US Passat. A 2018 sedan is in none of them. Secondary sources describing a 2017 Passat wagon are repeating a model-year label on a car whose production dates are 2005-2006.
Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and require every genuinely open campaign closed before purchase. Do not let a seller price this car around a Takata repair it does not need.
Est. cost: n/a Source: NHTSA Part 573 report 24V-834
