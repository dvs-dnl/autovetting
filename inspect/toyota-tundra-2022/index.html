What the VIN tells you

Do not read the 8th digit on a 3rd-gen Tundra — on this truck it is the body/cab type, not the engine. Toyota’s Part 565 filing for the TNGA-F Tundra (NHTSA MID doc 23-008) puts Engine Type in the 4th VIN digit and Trim Level in the 5th, which is a change from the older 5th-digit-engine layout used on the 2007–2021 trucks. That matters because all three variants share one engine family — the 3.4L V35A-FTS twin-turbo V6 (Toyota markets it as 3.5L; actual displacement is 3,445 cc) — and the 4th digit is what separates the three states that engine is sold in: two non-hybrid tunes and the i-FORCE MAX hybrid. Note that Toyota’s Part 565 filing lists engine-level ratings that do not match the marketed figures (the hybrid is advertised at 437 hp combined system output), so match the digit, not the horsepower. So you cannot tell an i-FORCE MAX from a non-hybrid by looking at the engine badge or the 8th digit, and a mis-sold hybrid is the most expensive way to get this wrong. Decode the full VIN at autovetting.com/decode and confirm hybrid status against the window sticker before applying the hybrid-specific checks below. Then run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls — 2022 was the first year of this platform and early production carried multiple campaigns.
