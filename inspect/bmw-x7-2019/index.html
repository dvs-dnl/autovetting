The X7 is BMW flagship 3-row SUV — imposing presence, genuine luxury interior, and strong powertrain options. The B58 inline-six is excellent; the N63 V8 has documented oil consumption and injector issues. On any used X7: BMW maintenance costs are unforgiving, extended warranty or CPO is highly recommended, and the B58 is strongly preferred over the N63 for ownership cost. Only buy with complete BMW dealer service history.

Engine

B58 I6 (xDrive40i) — excellent engine

Low Risk
The B58 is one of the best modern BMW engines — reliable, smooth, and tunable. Oil leak watch on valve cover at high mileage.
Check valve cover and oil filter housing for seeps. Cold-start smooth idle expected.
Est. cost: $400–$700 valve cover if seeping Source: BMW B58 reliability data

N63 V8 (xDrive50i, and the 2020+ M50i) — oil consumption + injectors

High Risk
The N63 is notorious for high oil consumption, injector failures, and cooling system issues. BMW issued an extended warranty on some N63 units. Factor $3,000–$8,000 in potential deferred maintenance.
Check dipstick immediately. N63 consuming >1 qt per 1,000 miles is a known problem. Ask for all N63 Customer Care Package history.
Est. cost: $3,000–$8,000 N63 deferred maintenance package Source: BMW N63 reliability pattern

Cooling System

Water pump and thermostat (B58 and N63)

High Risk at 60k+
BMW electric water pumps and thermostats fail on schedule. Both B58 and N63 need service around 60k–80k miles.
Test heat output at idle and confirm temperature gauge stable. Any temp fluctuation = pump service due.
Est. cost: $600–$1,200 pump + thermostat Source: BMW water pump pattern

Maintenance Cost

BMW service requirement — X7 is expensive

High Risk without records
X7 dealer services run $1,500–$3,000 per interval. Without documented BMW service history, assume $5,000–$10,000 deferred maintenance backlog.
Request ALL BMW dealer service records. No records = walk away or negotiate aggressively.

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

25V-644
Starter Motor Overheats - fire risk (supersedes the software fix)
2019-2020 X7 among six BMW lines, 145,102 vehicles: after repeated start attempts the starter motor can overheat from electrical overload and ignite combustible material in the engine bay. Dealer replaces the engine starter. This campaign explicitly includes cars already repaired under 24V-576, which got software only and need the hardware too - so an X7 whose history shows the 2024 starter recall as completed still needs this one, and both have to read closed. Owner letters went out 2026-02-09, VINs searchable from 2025-11-17.
24V-576
Starter Motor Overheats - the earlier software-only remedy
2019-2020 X7 and other BMW lines: same starter overheat and fire mechanism, remedied in 2024 with a vehicle software update rather than a new starter. BMW later concluded that was not enough, which is why 25V-644 exists and replaces the hardware. Listed so a buyer reading a service record can tell which of the two was actually done.
19V-513
High-Pressure Fuel Pump Flange Cracks - fuel leak
The high-pressure fuel pump may not have been properly attached to the engine, so its flange can crack and leak fuel near hot engine components. Dealer replaces the high-pressure fuel pump. Any fuel smell after a hot shutdown on an unverified car makes this the first VIN check to run, not the last.
21V-062
Integrated Brake System Weld - loss of ABS and brake assist
The rotor and shaft inside the integrated brake system may not be properly welded and can separate under hard braking, costing ABS and most of the braking assistance. Dealer replaces the whole integrated brake system. Owner letters went out 2021-05-25.
19V-541
Left-Side Wheel Bolts Under-Tightened - wheel can detach
2019 X5 xDrive50i and X7 xDrive40i: left-side wheel bolts may have been insufficiently tightened and can loosen over time until a wheel comes off. Only 5 vehicles were ever in scope, so the odds any given X7 is one of them are negligible - it is listed because the consequence is a detached wheel and the VIN check is free.
19V-386
Head Airbag Misaligned With Trim
2019 X7 xDrive40i and xDrive50i: the head airbags may not have been aligned with the trim during installation, which can impair deployment. Dealer inspects and reinstalls the head airbags where needed. Nothing about this is visible from the seat, so it is a VIN-only item.
22V-670
Third-Row Backrest Lock Springs
2019-2022 X7 with third-row seats: the compression springs that lock the backrest may be installed wrong or come loose, so the backrest may not fully lock in a crash. Dealer inspects the third-row seat and reinstalls or replaces the springs. Owner letters went out 2022-11-04.
19V-135
Business Lounge Seat Adapter Bolts
2019 X7 ordered with the business lounge seat option only: the seat adapter attachment bolts may not have been properly tightened, raising occupant injury risk in a crash. Dealer replaces the bolts. Unsold cars were quarantined on dealer lots at the time, so most of these were fixed before first delivery - check the option list before assuming it applies.
21V-096
Reverse Camera Image Obscured or Screen Dark
Shifting into reverse can leave part of the rearview image obscured or the screen unlit, an FMVSS 111 failure. Dealer inspects the rearview camera software and updates it. Worth testing on the drive: select reverse, wait, and watch whether the full image appears every time.
19V-684
Back-Up Camera Setting Persists Between Drives
The back-up camera display settings can be changed so the rear image disappears, and the system keeps that setting the next time the car is in reverse - an FMVSS 111 failure. Dealer updates the back-up camera software. A seller may have set it that way without knowing, so a blank reverse screen is a software question before it is a camera question.
22V-267
Sunroof Can Close With No Key Fob Inside
The roof function control unit may be programmed incorrectly, letting the sunroof close without the key fob present - an FMVSS 118 failure, which is the anti-entrapment standard. Dealer reprograms the control unit. Owner letters went out 2022-06-20.

What the VIN tells you

On the G07 X7 the 8th VIN digit is not the engine character — the same thing that is true of the G05 X5, and the most useful thing to know before decoding one. Verified against NHTSA vPIC: position 8 reads ‘5’ on both engines, on partial decodes and on real-world VINs alike (5UXCW2C59KL086231 and 5UXCX4C5XKLS38106). The engine rides in positions 5–6, inside the model-designation block: ‘W2’ returns X7 / 2019 / xDrive40i / 3.0L / 6 cylinders / 335 hp — the B58 inline-six — and ‘X4’ returns xDrive50i / 4.4L / 8 cylinders / 456 hp, the N63 V8. That fork is the whole risk story on this truck: the N63 items below are the difference between budgeted BMW running costs and a $3,000–$8,000 deferred-maintenance bill, and a tailgate badge is the easiest thing on the car to change, so read positions 5–6 off the actual VIN rather than off the listing. One year tell worth recognizing: ‘X6’ in those positions returns nothing on a 2019 year character, while 5UXCX6C07L9B26218 decodes clean as a 2020 X7 M50i, 4.4L, 523 hp — so an M50i is a 2020-or-later car, and 456 hp is the most V8 a 2019 came with. And per house rule: a decode returning a configuration is evidence of what the VIN encodes, not proof the car was built or sold that way — use it to identify the car in front of you, not to argue availability.
