2017 Chrysler Pacifica
Touring / Touring L / Limited / Pinnacle / Hybrid (RU, 1st gen, 2017–2022)
High Attention

The first-generation Pacifica is the most car-like minivan to drive and the only one offering a plug-in hybrid, but it carries real used-buyer risk that rewards a careful inspection. The 948TE 9-speed automatic can shift roughly and is the subject of an NHTSA stall investigation; the 3.6L Pentastar's water pump and oil-cooler are known early failures that can escalate to a head gasket on an all-aluminum engine; and the Pacifica Hybrid is tied to a high-voltage battery fire recall that has been re-issued twice. A well-documented gas Touring L with a clean transmission and verified recalls is a genuinely good family van; a neglected one — or any Hybrid without the fire-recall remedy confirmed — is a walk-away.

Powertrain — 3.6L Pentastar V6 & 9-Speed

948TE 9-speed automatic — shift quality + stall investigation

High Attention
The ZF-derived 948TE 9-speed can exhibit harsh or hesitant shifts, gear hunting, and a lag from Park to a drive gear. NHTSA has investigated an internal transmission wiring-connector short that can cause an unexpected engine/transmission shutdown. Early 2017 builds saw the most complaints; multiple software updates were issued.
Test drive through the full range: gentle 5–35 mph tip-in, a highway on-ramp, and several Park-to-Drive/Reverse engagements. Feel for clunks, flares, or a multi-second delay engaging a gear. Scan for transmission codes and confirm the latest TCM software is installed at a dealer (free VIN check).
Est. cost: $0 software / $350–$550 fluid service / $3,000–$5,000 valve body or unit if mechanical Source: NHTSA stall investigation + FCA transmission software campaigns

3.6L Pentastar water pump + integrated oil cooler

High Attention
The Pentastar's water pump and the integrated oil-cooler/filter housing are documented early failures. Because the engine is all-aluminum, even a minor overheat from a failed pump can warp the head and trigger a head-gasket failure — far costlier than the pump itself.
Check coolant level and look for crusty residue or weeping at the front of the engine and around the oil-filter housing. Confirm the temperature gauge holds at the midpoint on the test drive. Ask whether the water pump has been replaced (common by 60k–90k miles).
Est. cost: $700–$1,100 water pump / $400–$900 oil-cooler housing / $2,500–$4,000 if a head gasket follows an overheat Source: Pentastar V6 documented failure pattern (RepairPal + owner forums)

Pentastar rocker-arm / lifter tick + oil maintenance

Monitor
Pentastar V6s can develop a rocker-arm/lifter tick and are sensitive to oil neglect (sludge in the cooler and VVT solenoids). A cold-start tick that fades is usually benign; a persistent tick with a misfire code is not.
Cold-start the engine and listen for a tick that persists past 30 seconds. Scan for misfire codes (P0300–P0306). Demand oil-change records — intervals beyond 8,000 miles raise the risk.
Est. cost: $0 if maintained / $600–$1,500 rocker/lifter work Source: Pentastar service pattern

Pacifica Hybrid / PHEV (if equipped)

High-voltage battery FIRE recall — 22V-077 / 24V-536 / 24V-538

Critical
Pacifica Hybrid (plug-in) models are tied to a high-voltage battery fire risk that can occur while parked and off. Chrysler's 2022 recall (22V-077) did not fully resolve it; NHTSA reopened the issue and Stellantis issued expanded/replacement recalls in 2024 — 24V-536 for 2017–2018 PHEVs and 24V-538 for 2019–2021 PHEVs — citing a folded/torn anode tab that can short the pack. Interim guidance told owners not to charge and to park away from structures until repaired.
On any Hybrid, run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and confirm the latest battery recall remedy (24V-536 or 24V-538) is COMPLETE — not just the superseded 22V-077. Walk away from any Hybrid that has not received the final battery inspection/replacement remedy.
Est. cost: $0 dealer remedy / walk away if unrepaired Source: NHTSA 22V-077, 24V-536, 24V-538

Plug-in charging + electric range health

Monitor
A healthy Pacifica Hybrid should deliver roughly 25–32 miles of electric range; significant degradation or charging faults point to battery or onboard-charger problems.
Charge the van and confirm it reaches a near-full electric-range estimate, then drive it in EV mode. Verify the charge cord and port work and that no hybrid-system warning lights are present.
Est. cost: $0 if healthy / $400–$2,000+ charger or module faults Source: Pacifica Hybrid owner data

12V auxiliary battery + hybrid wake-up

Monitor
Hybrids rely on a 12V system to boot the high-voltage controllers; a weak 12V battery causes intermittent no-starts and spurious electrical faults that mimic bigger problems.
Load-test the 12V battery and confirm the van wakes and powers up without warning lights.
Est. cost: $150–$300 12V battery Source: Hybrid 12V pattern

Electrical & Infotainment

Uconnect freeze / reboot / ghost-touch

Monitor
Uconnect 4/4C systems on the Pacifica are a top complaint area: random reboots, frozen screens, backup-camera dropouts, and phantom touch inputs. Most are addressed by software but some need a head-unit replacement.
Cycle every infotainment function — radio, Apple CarPlay/Android Auto, backup camera, climate, rear-seat screens if equipped. Confirm the backup camera appears within two seconds of reverse and the screen does not freeze.
Est. cost: $0 software update / $1,200–$2,500 head unit if hardware Source: NHTSA electrical complaints + FCA software campaigns

Power sliding door motors, cables + tracks

Monitor
The dual power sliding doors are high-use mechanisms; motor/cable wear and track debris cause slow operation, mid-travel reversal, and failure to latch — a frequent and visible Pacifica complaint.
Cycle each sliding door fully five times from the handle, the B-pillar button, the dash switch, and the key fob. Watch for hesitation, grinding, or failure to latch on the first attempt.
Est. cost: $400–$800 per door motor / $100 track cleaning Source: Pacifica sliding-door complaint pattern

Power liftgate struts + motor

Low Risk
By 5–7 years the power liftgate can lose strut pressure or develop a balky motor, causing slow travel or a door that drifts.
Open and close the power liftgate from all controls; open it halfway and confirm it holds position.
Est. cost: $80–$160 struts / $300–$600 motor Source: Standard wear item

Stow 'n Go & Interior (gas models)

Stow 'n Go bin water intrusion

Monitor
A recurring Pacifica complaint: water collects in the second-row Stow 'n Go floor bins after rain or a car wash, leading to damp carpet, mildew smell, and corrosion. Sources include door/seal leaks and clogged drains. (Hybrids have no Stow 'n Go to check.)
Lift the Stow 'n Go bin lids and feel for standing water, dampness, or a musty smell. Check the carpet edges and under the front-door sills for staining.
Est. cost: $150–$400 seal/drain repair / more if carpet or modules were soaked Source: Pacifica forum + NHTSA complaint pattern

HVAC blend-door actuator click

Monitor
A clicking or knocking from behind the dash with inconsistent temperature usually points to a failing blend-door actuator — common across FCA products.
Run the climate control through hot, cold, and each vent mode; listen for a repetitive click behind the dash.
Est. cost: $250–$500 actuator (labor-driven) Source: FCA HVAC actuator pattern

Second/third-row seats & latches

Low Risk
Exercise the folding/Stow 'n Go (gas) or removable seat hardware and all latches and seatbelts.
Fold and raise every available row, and tug-test all seatbelts and child-seat anchors.
Est. cost: $50–$300 latch/cable repair Source: Standard wear item

Suspension, Brakes & Body

Front suspension clunk / sway-bar links

Low Risk
Sway-bar end links and front strut mounts wear on a heavy van and produce a clunk over bumps.
Drive over rough pavement and listen for a front-end clunk; have the links and mounts checked on a lift.
Est. cost: $150–$350 links / $300–$600 strut mounts Source: Common wear pattern

Brake wear (heavy van; hybrid regen differs)

Monitor
The Pacifica is heavy; gas models can wear front brakes faster than expected, while Hybrids use regenerative braking that can leave rear pads under-worn but rotors rusty from light use.
Inspect pad thickness and rotor condition front and rear; on a Hybrid, check the rear rotors for heavy rust from regen-dominant braking.
Est. cost: $300–$600 per axle brake job Source: Vehicle-weight + hybrid regen pattern

Documentation & Recalls

Manual park-release / rollaway recall 18V-395

High Attention
2017–2018 Pacificas were recalled (NHTSA 18V-395, FCA U50) because the manual park-release cover could be removed by hand, allowing the van to be shifted out of Park and roll away. Roughly 240,000 vans were affected.
Confirm 18V-395 is complete via the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls on any 2017–2018 van.
Est. cost: Free dealer remedy Source: NHTSA 18V-395

Full recall verification + service history

Critical
Between the PHEV fire campaigns, the rollaway recall, and the Pentastar's sensitivity to oil neglect, an unverified Pacifica carries stacked risk. Service history is the single best predictor of which Pacifica is a good buy.
Run the VIN at nhtsa.gov/recalls and require every open campaign to show 'remedy complete.' Demand oil-change and any transmission-service records; a Hybrid with no fire-recall remedy, or a gas van with no oil history, is a walk-away.
Est. cost: Walk away from unrepaired safety recalls Source: NHTSA recall database

What the VIN tells you

On the Pacifica the single most important decode is gas vs. plug-in hybrid, because they are mechanically different vans. The conventional models use the 3.6L Pentastar V6 (287 hp) paired with the 948TE 9-speed automatic — the 8th VIN digit is 'G' for this Pentastar. The Pacifica Hybrid (a plug-in, badged 'Hybrid') uses an Atkinson-cycle 3.6L with a dual-motor eFlite EVT and a 16 kWh battery — a different 8th-digit code, and it is the variant tied to the high-voltage battery fire recalls (22V-077, then the 24V-536 / 24V-538 expansions). A Hybrid has no second-row Stow 'n Go (the battery lives in that floor well), so if the second-row seats fold into the floor you are looking at a gas van. Never trust the badge alone — decode the full VIN at autovetting.com/decode (NHTSA vPIC) to confirm engine family and whether a fire recall applies. The 10th VIN digit gives the model year: H=2017, J=2018, K=2019, L=2020, M=2021, N=2022.
