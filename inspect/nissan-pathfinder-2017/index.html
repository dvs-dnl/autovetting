2017 Nissan Pathfinder
S / SV / SL / Platinum (R52, 4th gen, 2013-2020)
High Attention

The R52 Pathfinder replaced the 4WD body-on-frame model with an FWD-based unibody crossover — a controversial move. The CVT paired with the 3.5L V6 on a heavy 3-row SUV is the headline concern: the transmission is under more load than in lighter vehicles, and failure has been documented earlier than on Altima/Rogue. Radiator coolant mixing with CVT fluid (via a cross-contamination failure) is the most catastrophic documented issue.

Transmission — Most Critical

CVT radiator cross-contamination check

Critical
The R52 Pathfinder has a known failure mode where the transmission oil cooler inside the radiator leaks coolant into the CVT fluid, destroying the CVT. Pink/milky CVT fluid or pink coolant in the reservoir = catastrophic failure in progress.
Pull CVT dipstick and inspect color — should be clear reddish-amber. Any pink, milky, or foamy appearance = walk away immediately. Also check coolant reservoir for pink or brown discoloration.
Est. cost: $3,500–$5,500 CVT + $800–$1,500 radiator replacement Source: NHTSA class action + Nissan TSB NTB13-118

CVT fluid service history (NS-3)

High Attention
Even without contamination, the CVT under V6 3-row SUV load requires NS-3 fluid every 30k miles.
Ask for all CVT fluid change receipts.
Est. cost: $200–$350 flush / $3,500–$5,500 CVT Source: Nissan service interval

Engine

VQ35DE oil consumption

Monitor
VQ35 can consume oil above 100k miles with extended intervals.
Check dipstick before driving.
Est. cost: Monitor Source: VQ35 pattern

Cooling System

Radiator condition and coolant color

Critical
Radiator is the source of the CVT contamination problem. Inspect carefully for any leaks.
Inspect radiator for leaks. Check coolant color — should be green or orange, never pink or milky.
Est. cost: $800–$1,500 radiator replacement Source: Nissan TSB NTB13-118

Electrical

Low Risk

Documentation

CVT + radiator service history — walk-away items

Critical
The CVT contamination issue and fluid service history are deal-breakers without documentation.
Run nhtsa.gov/recalls. Inspect CVT dipstick and coolant BEFORE any test drive.
Est. cost: Walk away from any contamination evidence Source: NHTSA + Nissan TSB

NHTSA Recalls (verify completion via VIN)

16V-244
OCS Software (2013–2016)
Passenger airbag may be incorrectly disabled — campaign covers 2013–2016 Pathfinder; verify on those years.
18V-601
ABS Actuator Brake-Fluid Leak (Fire Risk)
ABS actuator pump can leak brake fluid onto its circuit board — electrical short and fire risk. 2017-2018 Pathfinder. If the ABS light stays on 10+ seconds, park outdoors. Dealer inspects/replaces the actuator.
19V-807
ABS Actuator — Expansion / Re-Recall
Expands 18V-601 and re-covers vehicles previously inspected without repair (2017-2019 Pathfinder). Confirm BOTH campaigns closed by VIN, not just the first.
