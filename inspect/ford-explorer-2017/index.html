2017 Ford Explorer

The 5th-gen Explorer was a long-running and popular platform but had notable issues. The main problem is documented exhaust fume intrusion into the cabin on 2011–2017 models, which Ford addressed with a TSB but never issued a recall on. The 3.5L EcoBoost has Phase 1 and Phase 2 timing chain issues on early builds. The 2.3T EcoBoost is the more reliable choice for daily use. 2018–2019 refinements improved quality. Verify the exhaust/HVAC recall status carefully.

Exhaust Fume Intrusion — Safety Issue

Carbon monoxide / exhaust in cabin (2011–2017)

Critical — Verify Remedied
NHTSA investigated Ford Explorer for exhaust fumes entering the cabin through HVAC. Ford issued TSB 17-0064 addressing drainage and HVAC but no mandatory recall. This is a serious CO exposure risk.
Test HVAC on recirculate. Any exhaust smell with windows closed = do not purchase until dealer verifies TSB 17-0064 has been completed.
Est. cost: Free dealer TSB repair if completed; $0–$1,000 if not Source: NHTSA Explorer exhaust investigation / TSB 17-0064

Engine — Timing Chain (3.5L EcoBoost)

3.5L EcoBoost timing chain (early builds)

High Risk on pre-2016
3.5L EcoBoost Explorers have documented timing chain stretch on Phase 1 engine builds (pre-2016 approximate). Cold-start rattle = chain worn.
Cold-start with hood open. Any chain rattle = budget $2,000–$3,500 for repair or walk away.
Est. cost: $2,000–$3,500 timing chain service Source: Ford 3.5L EcoBoost chain pattern

2.3T EcoBoost — better choice for reliability

Low Risk
The 2.3T is the more reliable engine option for daily drivers with fewer chain and cooling concerns.
Standard oil and cold-start check.
Est. cost: Routine maintenance Source: Ford 2.3T EcoBoost reliability

Cooling System

Coolant leak (3.5L EcoBoost and V6)

Monitor
Water pump and radiator hoses are common failure points at 80k+ miles.
Inspect coolant reservoir level and hoses for seeping. Test cabin heat output at idle.
Est. cost: $400–$900 Source: Ford Explorer cooling pattern
